## How to use this calculator

The results update as you type.

- **Monthly investment:** how much you'll invest through your SIP each month.
- **Expected return:** the yearly return you expect. Mutual fund returns aren't guaranteed, so try a few rates.
- **Time period:** how many years you'll keep investing.
- **Yearly step-up:** optional; raises your monthly SIP by this percentage each year.

The results show the estimated value, how much you invest, the estimated returns and how many times your money grows. Below the calculator, the chart shows the value at the end of each year, split into invested amount and returns.

## Example: ₹10,000 a month for 15 years

Say you invest ₹10,000 a month for 15 years and expect a 12% return a year.

- **Invested:** ₹18,00,000.
- **Estimated value:** **₹50,45,760**.
- **Estimated returns:** ₹32,45,760, so your money grows about 2.8 times.

Changes to the amount, time and return make a big difference:

| SIP | Return | Years | Invested | Estimated value |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ₹10,000 a month | 12% | 10 | ₹12,00,000 | ₹23,23,390.76 |
| ₹10,000 a month | 12% | 15 | ₹18,00,000 | ₹50,45,760 |
| ₹10,000 a month | 10% | 15 | ₹18,00,000 | ₹41,79,242.66 |
| ₹5,000 a month | 12% | 20 | ₹12,00,000 | ₹49,95,739.60 |
| ₹25,000 a month | 12% | 25 | ₹75,00,000 | ₹4,74,40,877.31 |

**With a 10% yearly step-up:** starting at ₹10,000 a month and raising it by 10% each year for 15 years at 12%, you'd invest ₹38,12,697.80 and could have ₹86,83,849.43.

## How it's calculated

For a fixed monthly SIP, the value after n months is:

**FV = P × ((1 + i)^n − 1) ÷ i × (1 + i)**

- **P** is the monthly investment and **n** the number of months.
- **i** is the monthly rate: expected yearly return ÷ 12 ÷ 100. At 12%, i = 1% a month.
- Each investment is made at the start of the month, so it earns a full month's return.

With a step-up, the monthly amount rises by that percentage at the start of each new year, and the calculator adds up each month's investment and growth.

Real returns vary from month to month and can be negative. Expense ratios, exit loads and tax on gains aren't included. Figures are calculated at full precision and rounded for display.

## Tips for your SIP

1. **Start early.** Time does more than the amount: a smaller SIP started earlier can match a larger one started later.
2. **Step up with your income.** Raising your SIP each year adds up quickly.
3. **Stay invested through ups and downs.** SIPs buy more units when prices fall, which can help over long periods.
4. **Choose funds that match your goal.** Equity funds suit long goals; debt funds may suit shorter ones.
5. **Check costs.** A direct plan usually has a lower expense ratio than a regular plan.
6. **Review once a year.** Check your fund's performance against its benchmark and your goal.
