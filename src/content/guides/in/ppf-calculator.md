## How to use this calculator

The results update as you type.

- **Yearly deposit:** how much you'll put in each financial year, from ₹500 to ₹1,50,000.
- **Interest rate:** set to the current rate. Change it to test other rates.
- **Time period:** 15 years for the full term, or longer with 5-year extensions.
- **How you deposit:** once a year by 5 April, or every month by the 5th.

The results show your maturity amount, total deposits and interest earned. Below the calculator, the chart shows your balance each year, split into deposits and interest.

## Example: ₹1.5 lakh a year for 15 years

Say you deposit the maximum of ₹1,50,000 by 5 April every year at 7.1%.

- **Total deposits:** ₹22,50,000 over 15 years.
- **Interest earned:** ₹18,18,209.22.
- **Maturity amount:** **₹40,68,209.22**.

If you deposit ₹12,500 every month by the 5th instead, the maturity amount is ₹39,44,599.22, because each monthly deposit earns interest only from its month.

## How it's calculated

PPF interest is worked out monthly on the lowest balance between the 5th and the end of each month, and credited at the end of the financial year (31 March). The rate is compounded yearly.

**One deposit by 5 April:** the whole deposit earns interest for the year:

**Interest = (balance + deposit) × rate**

**Monthly deposits by the 5th:** each month's deposit earns interest from that month, so the year's interest is:

**Interest = rate × balance + rate ÷ 12 × monthly deposit × 78**

(78 is 12 + 11 + … + 1, the number of monthly deposits held across the year.)

The calculator holds the rate at today's rate for the whole term, although the government sets it every quarter. Figures are calculated at full precision and rounded for display.

## Tips for your PPF

1. **Deposit by 5 April.** A lump sum at the start of the financial year earns interest for all 12 months.
2. **If you pay monthly, pay by the 5th.** Deposits after the 5th miss that month's interest.
3. **Keep the account active.** Deposit at least ₹500 every year to avoid a penalty.
4. **Use the tax benefit if you're in the old regime.** Deposits can count towards your ₹1,50,000 deduction limit.
5. **Plan for the lock-in.** PPF is a 15-year account; partial withdrawals are allowed only after a few years.
6. **Extend if you don't need the money.** Extensions in 5-year blocks keep your savings earning tax-free interest.
