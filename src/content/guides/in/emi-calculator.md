## How to use this calculator

The results update as you type.

- **Loan amount:** how much you'll borrow.
- **Interest rate:** the yearly rate your lender quotes.
- **Loan tenure:** the time to repay, in years. Decimals work: 2.5 is 2 years 6 months.

The results show your monthly EMI, the total interest, the total you'll repay and the share that's interest. Below the calculator, the chart shows the principal and interest you pay each year, and the schedule gives the balance at the end of each year.

## Example: a ₹25 lakh home loan

Say you borrow ₹25,00,000 at 8.5% a year for 20 years.

- **EMI:** **₹21,695.58** a month for 240 months.
- **Total interest:** ₹27,06,939.40.
- **Total payment:** ₹52,06,939.40, so interest is about 52% of what you repay.

How the tenure and rate change it:

| --- | --- | --- |
| ₹25 lakh, 8.5%, 20 years | ₹21,695.58 | ₹27,06,939.40 |
| ₹25 lakh, 8.5%, 15 years | ₹24,618.49 | ₹19,31,328.01 |
| ₹25 lakh, 9%, 20 years | ₹22,493.15 | ₹28,98,355.74 |
| ₹50 lakh, 8.5%, 30 years | ₹38,445.67 | ₹88,40,442.70 |

For shorter loans: a car loan of ₹8 lakh at 9.5% for 5 years has an EMI of ₹16,801.49 and ₹2,08,089.34 of interest. A personal loan of ₹5 lakh at 11% for 3 years has an EMI of ₹16,369.36 and ₹89,296.91 of interest.

## How it's calculated

The EMI comes from the standard reducing-balance formula:

**EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n ÷ ((1 + r)^n − 1)**

- **P** is the loan amount, **n** the number of monthly EMIs and **r** the monthly rate: yearly rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100. At 8.5%, r is about 0.708% a month.
- Each month, interest = balance × r. The rest of the EMI reduces the balance.
- The last EMI is adjusted so the balance ends at exactly 0.

The calculator assumes the rate stays the same for the whole tenure. Many home loans have floating rates linked to an external benchmark, so the EMI or tenure can change. Figures are calculated at full precision and rounded for display.

## Tips for your loan

1. **Compare the total cost, not just the EMI.** A longer tenure lowers the EMI but adds interest.
2. **Check the effective cost.** Processing fees and insurance add to the cost; ask for the annual percentage rate.
3. **Prepay when you can.** Part-prepayments early in the loan save the most interest. Check any prepayment charges first.
4. **Keep EMIs affordable.** Leave room in your budget for a rate rise if your loan is on a floating rate.
5. **Use tax benefits where they apply.** Home loan principal and interest may qualify for deductions under the old tax regime.
6. **Review your rate.** If rates fall, ask your lender to reset your rate or consider a balance transfer.
