  - q: How are Canadian mortgage payments calculated?
    a: Fixed-rate mortgage interest in Canada is compounded semi-annually, so the rate is converted into a rate per payment before the standard payment formula is applied. That's why a Canadian payment is slightly lower than a US-style calculation at the same rate. Variable-rate mortgages usually compound monthly.
  - q: What is the payment on a $500,000 mortgage in Canada?
    a: At 5% fixed over 25 years, the monthly payment on $400,000 is $2,326.42, so $500,000 is about $2,908. Your own figure depends on your rate, amortization and payment frequency.
  - q: What is the minimum down payment in Canada?
    a: 5% of the first $500,000 of the price, plus 10% of the portion between $500,000 and $1.5 million. Homes of $1.5 million or more need at least 20% down. For a $600,000 home, the minimum is $35,000.
  - q: How much is CMHC insurance?
    a: With less than 20% down, you pay a one-time premium on the loan of 2.80% (15% to 19.99% down), 3.10% (10% to 14.99% down) or 4.00% (5% to 9.99% down). It's usually added to your mortgage. An amortization over 25 years adds 0.20%. Ontario, Quebec and Saskatchewan charge provincial sales tax on the premium, paid at closing.
  - q: Can I get a 30-year mortgage in Canada?
    a: With less than 20% down, the longest amortization is 25 years, or 30 years if you're a first-time buyer or buying a newly built home. With 20% or more down, insurance isn't required and lenders set their own maximum.
  - q: Do accelerated bi-weekly payments save money?
    a: Yes. An accelerated bi-weekly payment is half the monthly payment, paid 26 times a year, so you pay the equivalent of one extra monthly payment each year. In our example, that clears the mortgage about 3 years 4 months sooner and saves $55,761.05 in interest.
  - q: What is the difference between the term and the amortization?
    a: The amortization is the total time to pay off the mortgage, such as 25 years. The term is how long your current rate and conditions last, often 5 years. At the end of each term, you renew for a new term at the rate available then.

## How to use this calculator

The results update as you type.

- **Your home:** the purchase price and your down payment. Tick the box if you're a first-time buyer or buying a newly built home, which allows a 30-year amortization with less than 20% down.
- **Your mortgage:** the interest rate, whether it's fixed or variable, the amortization (the total time to pay it off), how often you'll pay, and the term (how long your rate is set for).

The results show your payment, the mortgage amount including any CMHC insurance premium, the total interest, and what you'll owe at renewal. If your down payment is too small for the price, you'll see the minimum. Below the calculator, the chart shows the principal and interest you pay each year.

## Example: a $600,000 home with 10% down

Say you buy a $600,000 home with a $60,000 down payment, at a 4.5% fixed rate over 25 years.

- **Insurance:** your down payment is 10%, so you need mortgage loan insurance. The premium is 3.10% of the $540,000 loan, or $16,740, added to your mortgage of **$556,740**.
- **Payment:** **$3,081.41 a month**, with $367,681.60 of interest over 25 years.
- **After a 5-year term:** you'd still owe $488,797.61. You'd have paid $116,941.93 in interest and $67,942.39 off the balance.

The payment frequency changes the payment and, for accelerated payments, the total interest:

| Frequency | Payment | Paid off in | Total interest |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly | $3,081.41 | 25 years | $367,681.60 |
| Bi-weekly | $1,420.77 | 25 years | $366,758.68 |
| Accelerated bi-weekly | $1,540.70 | 21 years 8 months | $311,920.55 |
| Accelerated weekly | $770.35 | 21 years 8 months | $311,294.78 |

The down payment matters too. With the minimum of $35,000, the premium rises to 4.00% and the payment to $3,252.21 a month. With $120,000 (20%) down, there's no insurance and the payment is $2,656.67.

As a first-time buyer choosing 30 years, the premium is 3.30% ($17,820) and the payment falls to $2,812.62 a month, but total interest rises to $454,721.48.

## How it's calculated

**Rate per payment.** Fixed rates compound semi-annually, so for n payments a year:

**i = (1 + r ÷ 2)^(2 ÷ n) − 1**

where r is the annual rate. Variable rates use (1 + r ÷ 12)^(12 ÷ n) − 1.

**Payment.** For a mortgage M repaid over N payments:

**P = M × i ÷ (1 − (1 + i)^−N)**

Accelerated bi-weekly and weekly payments are the monthly payment ÷ 2 or ÷ 4. The last payment is adjusted so the balance ends at exactly 0.

**Minimum down payment:** 5% of the first $500,000 and 10% of the rest, or 20% for homes of $1.5 million or more.

**Mortgage loan insurance** (less than 20% down): CMHC's premium on the loan is 2.80% for a loan-to-value up to 85%, 3.10% up to 90% and 4.00% up to 95%, plus 0.20% for amortizations over 25 years. Other insurers set their own premiums.

The calculator assumes your rate stays the same for the whole amortization. Figures are calculated at full precision and rounded for display.

## Tips for your mortgage

1. **Check the minimum down payment early.** It steps up above $500,000, so a higher price needs more than 5% down.
2. **Compare accelerated payments.** If your budget allows, accelerated bi-weekly or weekly payments shorten the mortgage without a big change to each payment.
3. **Plan for renewal.** Your rate is only set for the term. Try a higher rate in the calculator to see how a renewal could change your payment.
4. **Weigh a longer amortization carefully.** Thirty years lowers the payment but adds interest and a higher insurance premium.
5. **Budget for closing costs.** Land transfer tax, legal fees and any provincial sales tax on the insurance premium are paid when you buy.
6. **Use your FHSA.** Savings in a First Home Savings Account can go toward your down payment.
