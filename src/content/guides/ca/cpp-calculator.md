## How to use this calculator

The results update as you type.

- **Monthly pension at 65:** your estimate from your Statement of Contributions in My Service Canada Account. If you don't have it, the average for new recipients is a starting point.
- **Age you'll start:** any whole age from 60 to 70.
- **Compare totals up to age:** the age you'd like to compare total payments at, such as your life expectancy.

The results show your monthly and yearly pension at your chosen start age, the break-even age compared with starting at 65, and the total you'd receive by your comparison age. Below the calculator, the chart compares running totals for starting at 60, 65 and 70, and the table shows every start age.

## Example: $858.34 a month at 65

Say your estimated pension at 65 is $858.34 a month, the average for new recipients in July 2026. Here's how the start age changes it, with totals up to age 87:

| Start age | Change | Monthly | Yearly | Total by 87 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 60 | −36% | $549.34 | $6,592.05 | $177,985.38 |
| 63 | −14.4% | $734.74 | $8,816.87 | $211,604.84 |
| 65 | 0% | $858.34 | $10,300.08 | $226,601.76 |
| 68 | +25.2% | $1,074.64 | $12,895.70 | $245,018.30 |
| 70 | +42% | $1,218.84 | $14,626.11 | $248,643.93 |

**Break-even ages compared with 65:**

- Starting at 60, the total from 65 catches up at about **73 years 11 months**.
- Starting at 70, you catch up with 65 at about **81 years 11 months**.

If you live beyond the break-even age, the later start pays more in total. With the maximum pension of $1,507.65 at 65, starting at 70 gives $2,140.86 a month.

## How it's calculated

**Monthly pension at your start age:**

- Before 65: pension at 65 × (1 − 0.006 × months before 65)
- After 65: pension at 65 × (1 + 0.007 × months after 65)

So starting at 60 (60 months early) is 36% lower, and starting at 70 (60 months late) is 42% higher.

**Total received** = monthly pension × 12 × (comparison age − start age).

**Break-even age** with 65: the age x where monthly pension × (x − start age) = pension at 65 × (x − 65).

Amounts are in today's dollars, since CPP rises with inflation each year. The calculator doesn't include income tax, the post-retirement benefit for working while receiving CPP, survivor benefits, or the Quebec Pension Plan. Figures are calculated at full precision and rounded for display.

## Tips for deciding when to start

1. **Get your own estimate.** Your Statement of Contributions shows what you've earned so far.
2. **Think about your health and family history.** The break-even age matters most if you expect a long retirement.
3. **Consider your other income.** If you can live on savings or work longer, delaying CPP can give you a larger pension for life.
4. **Look at OAS too.** Old Age Security can also be delayed for a higher payment.
5. **Factor in taxes and benefits.** A larger CPP pension is taxable and can affect income-tested benefits.
6. **Apply ahead of time.** Applying in advance gives Service Canada time to process your application before you want payments to start.
