  - q: What are the 2026 RRSP and TFSA limits?

  - q: Why does the calculator put more into the RRSP?
    a: To compare fairly, both start from the same before-tax income. An RRSP contribution is deducted from your income, so the whole amount goes in and you get a refund. With a TFSA, you pay tax first and contribute what's left.
  - q: Is RRSP money taxed when I take it out?
    a: Yes. RRSP withdrawals, or RRIF payments after you convert, are added to your taxable income in the year you take them. TFSA withdrawals are tax-free and don't count as income.

  - q: Can I use both?
    a: Yes. Many people use both, depending on their income each year and their goals. A TFSA is also flexible for shorter-term goals, because you can withdraw without tax and get the room back the next year.

## How to use this calculator

The results update as you type.

- **Your income:** your province, your employment income now, and the yearly income you expect in retirement, including CPP, OAS, pensions and planned RRSP withdrawals.
- **Your savings:** how much before-tax income you'll set aside each year, how many years until you withdraw, and the yearly return you expect.

The results show which account leaves you more after tax and by how much, the value of each after tax, and the tax rates used. Below the calculator, the chart shows both values over time.

## Example: $80,000 in Ontario

Say you earn $80,000 in Ontario and set aside $5,000 of before-tax income a year for 25 years, earning 6%. In retirement, you expect $50,000 a year of income.

- **Tax rate now:** a $5,000 RRSP deduction saves $1,482.50 of tax, which is 29.65%.
- **RRSP:** $5,000 a year grows to $290,781.91. Taxed at your 19.05% retirement rate, that's **$235,387.96** after tax.
- **TFSA:** $5,000 less tax is $3,517.50 a year, which grows to **$204,565.08**, all tax-free.

The RRSP leaves you **$30,822.88** more, because your tax rate falls from 29.65% to 19.05%.

The result depends on your tax rates:

| Income now | Retirement income | Tax rate now | Tax rate later | Better choice |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $50,000 | $50,000 | 22.05% | 19.05% | RRSP by $8,723.46 |
| $80,000 | $50,000 | 29.65% | 19.05% | RRSP by $30,822.88 |
| $80,000 | $90,000 | 29.65% | 29.65% | Equal |
| $120,000 ($10,000 a year) | $50,000 | 36.89% | 19.05% | RRSP by $103,757.80 |

In BC, the same $80,000 example gives a 28.2% rate now and 19.6% later, and the RRSP leaves $25,007.24 more.

## How it's calculated

**The same starting point.** Both accounts start from the same before-tax income, C, each year:

- **RRSP:** you contribute C. The deduction gives a refund, which is assumed to be reinvested, so the full C goes in.
- **TFSA:** you pay tax first, so you contribute C × (1 − tax rate now).

**Growth.** Deposits are made at the start of each year and grow at the same yearly return in both accounts.

**At withdrawal.** The TFSA is tax-free. The RRSP is taxed at your rate in retirement:

**RRSP after tax = RRSP value × (1 − tax rate later)**

**Tax rates.** The rate now is the tax your RRSP deduction actually saves, using 2026 federal and provincial tax, CPP and EI credits. The rate later is your combined marginal rate on your retirement income at today's brackets.

The calculator doesn't include OAS recovery tax, benefits that depend on income (such as the Guaranteed Income Supplement), or pension income splitting. Quebec isn't covered. Figures are calculated at full precision and rounded for display.

## Tips for choosing

1. **Compare your tax rates.** A higher rate now and a lower rate later favours the RRSP.
2. **Reinvest your refund.** The RRSP's advantage depends on putting the refund to work; spending it changes the comparison.
3. **Think about income-tested benefits.** RRSP withdrawals count as income and can reduce benefits like the Guaranteed Income Supplement; TFSA withdrawals don't.
4. **Keep flexibility in mind.** TFSA withdrawals add back to your room the next year, so a TFSA suits goals before retirement too.
5. **Check your room.** Your RRSP deduction limit is on your Notice of Assessment, and your TFSA room is in CRA My Account.
6. **Buying a first home?** An FHSA gives an RRSP-style deduction and TFSA-style tax-free withdrawals for a qualifying home.
