## How to use this calculator

The results update as you type.

- **Province:** where the property is. In Ontario, tick the box if it's in the City of Toronto; in Quebec, if it's in Montréal.
- **Purchase price:** what you're paying. In Quebec, New Brunswick and PEI, the tax uses the higher of the price and the assessed or market value, so add that value if it's higher.
- **About you:** tick the boxes if you're a first-time buyer (Ontario, BC and PEI), or buying a newly built home in BC. In Nova Scotia, enter your municipality's deed transfer tax rate.

The results show the tax to pay, the tax before rebates, any first-time buyer relief, and the share of the price. The breakdown lists each tax and rebate.

## Example: a $700,000 home

Here's the land transfer tax on a $700,000 home in each place, without first-time buyer relief:

| Where | Tax | Share of price |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ontario (outside Toronto) | $10,475 | 1.5% |
| Toronto | $20,950 | 3.0% |
| British Columbia | $12,000 | 1.71% |
| Quebec (provincial rates) | $8,610.50 | 1.23% |
| Montréal | $9,349 | 1.34% |
| Manitoba | $11,650 | 1.66% |
| Nova Scotia (Halifax, 1.5%) | $10,500 | 1.5% |
| New Brunswick | $7,000 | 1% |
| Prince Edward Island | $7,000 | 1% |

**First-time buyers:**

- **Ontario:** the $4,000 refund brings the tax down to $6,475.
- **Toronto:** both rebates ($4,000 + $4,475) bring $20,950 down to $12,475.
- **BC:** the $8,000 exemption brings $12,000 down to $4,000. A newly built home under $1.1 million is fully exempt.
- **PEI:** eligible first-time buyers pay nothing.

## How it's calculated

**Brackets (Ontario, Toronto, BC, Manitoba, Quebec).** Each rate applies only to the part of the price inside its bracket. For example, Ontario on $700,000:

- 0.5% × $55,000 = $275
- 1% × $195,000 = $1,950
- 1.5% × $150,000 = $2,250
- 2% × $300,000 = $6,000
- Total: **$10,475**

BC adds a further 2% on residential value over $3 million. Toronto adds higher rates on homes over $3 million.

**Flat rates.** New Brunswick and PEI charge 1% of the higher of the price and the assessed value. Nova Scotia municipalities charge their own rate on the price.

**First-time buyer relief.** Ontario refunds up to $4,000 and Toronto up to $4,475. BC exempts the full tax up to $500,000, then $8,000 up to $835,000, reducing in a straight line to nothing at $860,000. PEI exempts eligible first-time buyers.

You must meet each program's conditions, such as living in the home. Foreign buyer taxes, legal fees and registration fees aren't included. Figures are calculated at full precision and rounded for display.

## Tips for land transfer tax

1. **Budget for it early.** Land transfer tax is due on closing and usually can't be added to your mortgage.
2. **Check first-time buyer rules.** Each program has its own definition of a first-time buyer and conditions to meet.
3. **Know the value used.** In some provinces, the assessed or market value counts if it's higher than the price.
4. **Compare locations.** Buying just outside Toronto avoids the municipal tax.
5. **Ask your lawyer or notary.** They'll confirm the exact amount and claim any rebate when the transfer is registered.
6. **Plan for other closing costs.** Legal fees, title insurance and moving costs add up too.
