## How to use this calculator

The results update as you type.

- **Province or territory:** where you lived on December 31.
- **Employment income:** your yearly salary or wages before tax.
- **RRSP and FHSA deductions:** any contributions you'll deduct for 2026. Leave it at 0 if none.

The results show your take-home pay, total income tax, CPP and EI, and your marginal and average tax rates. Below the calculator, the chart shows where your income goes, and the table gives each figure per year, month, two weeks and week.

## Example: $80,000 in Ontario

Say you earn $80,000 in Ontario in 2026.

- **CPP:** $4,230.45 on earnings up to $74,600, plus $216 of second additional CPP, for $4,446.45. **EI:** $1,123.07.
- **Taxable income:** $79,073, after deducting the $927 of enhanced CPP contributions.
- **Federal tax:** $9,242.60. **Ontario tax:** $4,885.26, including the $750 Ontario Health Premium.
- **Take-home pay:** **$60,302.63**, or $5,025.22 a month.

Your marginal rate is 29.65% (20.5% federal plus 9.15% Ontario), and your average rate is 17.66%.

## How it's calculated

**CPP and EI.** CPP is 5.95% of earnings between $3,500 and $74,600 (4.95% base plus 1% first additional), plus 4% between $74,600 and $85,000. EI is 1.63% of earnings up to $68,900.

**Taxable income** = income − enhanced CPP contributions (the 1% and 4% parts) − RRSP and FHSA deductions.

**Federal tax** = tax on taxable income at the 2026 brackets, less 14% of these credits:

- the basic personal amount ($16,452, falling to $14,829 between $181,440 and $258,482)
- the Canada employment amount ($1,501)
- base CPP contributions and EI premiums

**Provincial tax** = tax at your province's brackets, less its lowest rate times its basic personal amount, base CPP and EI. Ontario then adds its surtax (on basic Ontario tax over $5,818) and the Ontario Health Premium (up to $900).

The calculator doesn't include provincial low-income tax reductions, other credits (such as medical expenses or donations), or income other than employment. Your employer's withholding uses payroll formulas, so your payslips may differ slightly. Figures are calculated at full precision and rounded for display.

## Tips for your income tax

1. **Use your marginal rate for decisions.** It shows how much of a raise you keep and how much an RRSP deduction saves.
2. **Compare RRSP and FHSA deductions.** Both reduce taxable income; an FHSA can also be used tax-free for a first home.
3. **Check your CPP and EI.** If you have two jobs, you may overpay CPP or EI and get it back when you file.
4. **Claim every credit you're entitled to.** Tuition, medical expenses, donations and others lower your tax further.
5. **Keep track of TFSA room.** TFSA contributions don't reduce your tax, but growth and withdrawals are tax-free.
6. **File on time.** The deadline for most people is April 30 of the following year.
