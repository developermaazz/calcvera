## How to use this calculator

The results update as you type.

- **HELP balance:** what you owe now. You can check it in ATO online services through myGov.
- **Repayment income:** your taxable income plus reportable super contributions, reportable fringe benefits and net investment losses.
- **Pay rise each year:** how much you expect your income to grow.
- **Indexation each year:** set to the latest rate applied by the ATO. Change it to test other rates.
- **Voluntary repayments:** any extra you'll pay each year.

The results show your compulsory repayment for 2026–27, how long your debt could take to repay, and the total repaid and indexation. Below the calculator, the chart shows your balance each year.

## Example: $25,000 owed on $85,000 income

Say you owe $25,000 and your repayment income is $85,000, rising 3% a year, with 2.8% indexation.

- **This year:** your compulsory repayment is **$2,320.80**, which is 15% of the $15,472 above $69,528.
- **First year's balance:** $25,000 plus $700 indexation, less $2,320.80, leaves $23,379.20.
- **Repaid in:** about **11 years**, repaying $29,506.41, of which $4,506.41 is indexation.

Income makes a big difference with the same $25,000 debt:

| Repayment income | Repayment this year | Repaid in | Total indexation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $70,000 | $70.80 | 47 years | $36,462.62 |
| $85,000 | $2,320.80 | 11 years | $4,506.41 |
| $100,000 | $4,570.80 | 6 years | $2,419.64 |
| $130,000 | $9,076.11 | 3 years | $1,381.49 |

Voluntary repayments of $2,000 a year on $85,000 income clear the debt in 6 years, with $2,207.70 of indexation instead of $4,506.41.

If your pay doesn't rise while the thresholds do, your repayments shrink over time and the debt may never be repaid in this projection.

## How it's calculated

**Compulsory repayment (2026–27):**

- nil up to $69,528 of repayment income
- 15% of repayment income over $69,528, up to $129,717
- $9,028 plus 17% of repayment income over $129,717, up to $186,050
- 10% of all repayment income from $186,051

**Each year in the projection:**

1. Voluntary repayments come off the balance.
2. On 1 June, indexation is added to the balance.
3. The compulsory repayment for the year is credited after your tax return is assessed.

Your income grows by your pay rise each year. As an assumption, the repayment thresholds rise by the indexation rate each year; the actual figures are set each year. Your employer may withhold more or less during the year, and the difference is settled when you lodge your tax return. Figures are calculated at full precision and rounded for display.

## Tips for your HELP debt

1. **Tell your employer you have a HELP debt.** They'll withhold enough to cover your compulsory repayment, so you don't get a bill at tax time.
2. **Check your balance each year.** Indexation is applied on 1 June, so voluntary repayments made before then reduce the amount indexed.
3. **Know what counts as income.** Salary sacrifice and fringe benefits are added back, so they don't lower your HELP repayment.
4. **Weigh voluntary repayments against other goals.** Paying off debts with higher interest or building savings may come first.
5. **Plan for a higher income.** Repayments rise quickly once your income passes the higher thresholds.
6. **Keep your details up to date.** Make sure your contact details are current in ATO online services.
