## How to use this calculator

The results update as you type.

- **Your loan:** the amount you'll borrow, the interest rate, the loan term and how often you'll repay. Add an interest-only period if your loan has one.
- **Pay it off faster:** an extra amount with each repayment, and your offset account balance.

The results show your repayment, the total interest and total repaid, and how long the loan takes to pay off. If you add an offset or extra repayments, you'll see how much interest and time they save. Below the calculator, the chart compares your balance each year with and without them.

## Example: $600,000 at 5.8% over 30 years

Say you borrow $600,000 at 5.8% over 30 years.

| Repayments | Repayment | Paid off in | Total interest |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly | $3,520.52 | 30 years | $667,386.56 |
| Fortnightly | $1,624.07 | 30 years | $666,776.40 |
| Weekly | $811.87 | 30 years | $666,514.85 |
| Half the monthly, fortnightly | $1,760.26 | 24 years 8 months | $528,610.94 |

Ways to pay less interest, with monthly repayments:

- **Offset account:** $50,000 in an offset saves **$190,153.02** and clears the loan in 25 years 6 months.
- **Extra repayments:** an extra $200 a month saves $100,938.42 and clears it in 26 years 2 months.

The rate matters too: at 5.5% the monthly repayment is $3,406.73, and at 6.5% it's $3,792.41.

**Interest-only:** with five years interest-only, you'd pay $2,900 a month at first, then $3,792.79 a month for the remaining 25 years. Total interest rises to $711,836.54.

## How it's calculated

Your repayment comes from the standard loan formula:

**M = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n)**

- **P** is the loan amount, **n** the number of repayments and **r** the rate per repayment: the annual rate ÷ 12, 26 or 52.
- Each repayment, interest = (balance − offset) × r. The rest of the repayment, and any extra, reduces the balance.
- **Half the monthly repayment fortnightly:** the monthly repayment ÷ 2, paid 26 times a year.
- **Interest-only:** the repayment is just the interest for that period. Then principal and interest repayments start over the rest of the term.

An offset balance and extra repayments don't change your scheduled repayment; they shorten the loan. The calculator assumes the rate stays the same for the whole term. Some lenders work out interest daily and charge it monthly, so your statements may differ slightly. Figures are calculated at full precision and rounded for display.

## Tips for your home loan

1. **Compare the comparison rate.** It includes most fees, so it's a better guide to the cost of a loan than the advertised rate.
2. **Try a higher rate.** Check you could still afford your repayments if rates rose by a couple of percentage points.
3. **Use an offset or redraw if it suits you.** Savings in an offset reduce interest while staying available, but some offset loans have higher rates or fees.
4. **Pay a little extra early.** Extra repayments in the first years save the most interest.
5. **Plan for the end of an interest-only period.** Repayments rise when principal and interest start.
6. **Review your loan regularly.** Ask your lender for a better rate, or compare refinancing costs with the savings.
