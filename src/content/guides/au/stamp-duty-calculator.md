## How to use this calculator

The results update as you type.

- **State or territory:** where the property is.
- **Property value:** the price, or the market value if it's higher.
- **Who's buying:** a first home buyer, someone buying a home to live in, or an investor.
- **New home:** in Queensland and South Australia, tick this if it's a new home that hasn't been lived in. First home buyers of eligible new homes pay no duty there.

The results show the stamp duty to pay, the duty at general rates, how much any concession saves, and the share of the value. A note explains which concession applied.

A few things stand out:

- **Victoria:** $750,000 is the top of the first home buyer concession, so full duty applies. At $700,000, a first home buyer would pay about $24,713.
- **South Australia:** first home buyers pay nothing on an eligible new home, but there's no relief on established homes.
- **NSW:** a first home buyer at $900,000 pays $19,593.50 instead of $34,687.

## How it's calculated

**General rates.** Each state charges a base amount plus a rate on the value above a threshold. For example, NSW for 2026–27 on $750,000:

- $11,602 for the first $387,000
- plus $4.50 for every $100 over $387,000: $16,335
- Total: **$27,937**

Queensland, Western Australia, South Australia, Tasmania and the ACT charge per $100 "or part of $100", so the amount over the threshold is rounded up to the next $100. Victoria charges 5.5% of the whole value between $960,000 and $2 million. The Northern Territory uses a formula up to $525,000, then a flat percentage of the whole value.

**Concessions included:**

- **NSW:** no duty to $800,000 for first home buyers; above that, duty at $1 million × (value − $800,000) ÷ $200,000.
- **Victoria:** principal place of residence rates to $550,000; first home buyers pay nothing to $600,000, then general duty × (value − $600,000) ÷ $150,000.
- **Queensland:** home concession rates; first home buyers deduct a concession amount for their price band; new homes are exempt for first home buyers.
- **WA:** first home owner rate (nil to $600,000, then $16.15 per $100 to $800,000); a concessional rate for homes worth $200,000 or less.
- **SA:** first home buyers of eligible new homes pay nothing.
- **ACT:** owner-occupier rates; eligible buyers under the Home Buyer Concession Scheme pay nothing from 1 July 2026.

Foreign purchaser surcharges, pensioner and off-the-plan concessions, vacant land and shared ownership aren't included. Figures are calculated at full precision; revenue offices may round to the dollar.

## Tips for stamp duty

1. **Check your eligibility.** First home buyer schemes have conditions, such as living in the home for a set time.
2. **Look at new homes.** In Queensland and South Australia, first home buyers of new homes pay no duty.
3. **Budget for duty upfront.** Stamp duty is usually due at or soon after settlement and can't always be added to your loan.
4. **Know the dutiable value.** It's normally the price, but the market value is used if that's higher.
5. **Ask your conveyancer.** They'll confirm the exact duty and lodge any concession application.
6. **Consider other costs.** Transfer and mortgage registration fees, inspections and legal fees add to the cost of buying.
