## How to use this calculator

Enter the numbers you know. The result updates as you type.

- **Monthly car payment budget:** the most you want to pay the lender each month.
- **Down payment:** cash you'll put down.
- **Trade-in value and amount owed:** what the dealer will give you for your current car, and what you still owe on it. The difference is your trade-in equity.
- **Interest rate (APR):** the rate you've been quoted or expect, based on your credit.
- **Loan term:** how many months you'll pay. Longer terms lower the payment but cost more interest.
- **Sales tax rate:** your state rate plus any local rate.
- **Fees:** dealer, title and registration fees. The calculator assumes you'll add them to the loan.
- **Sales tax after trade-in:** tick this if your state taxes the price after subtracting the trade-in value.

## Example: $500 a month over 60 months

Say you can pay $500 a month for 60 months at 7% APR. You have $3,000 for a down payment and a trade-in worth $5,000 with $2,000 still owed. Sales tax is 6%, charged after the trade-in, and fees are $800.

| Item | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Loan amount | $25,251.00 |
| Car price you can afford | $29,010 |
| Sales tax | $1,440.62 |
| Total interest | $4,749.00 |

The $500 payment supports a $25,251 loan. Add your $3,000 down payment and $3,000 of trade-in equity, take off the $800 in fees, and you have enough to cover a car priced up to about $29,010 plus its sales tax.

Now see what happens if you change the loan instead of the car:

- **72 months instead of 60:** the price you can afford rises to $32,856, but interest climbs to $6,672.78.
- **9% APR instead of 7%:** the price you can afford falls to $27,912, because more of each payment goes to interest.

A two-point difference in your rate costs about $1,100 of car. It's worth getting preapproved and comparing lenders before you visit a dealer.

## How it's calculated

First, the calculator works out the largest loan your payment can repay:

**Loan = budget × (1 − (1 + r)^−n) ÷ r**

Here, **r** is the monthly rate (APR ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and **n** is the number of monthly payments. At 0% APR, the loan is simply budget × n.

Then it adds the money you bring and removes the fees:

**Cash available = loan + down payment + (trade-in value − amount owed) − fees**

Finally, it solves for the price, allowing for sales tax:

- If tax is charged after the trade-in: **price = (cash available + tax rate × trade-in value) ÷ (1 + tax rate)**
- If tax is charged on the full price: **price = cash available ÷ (1 + tax rate)**

Total cost is everything you pay over the loan: the car price, sales tax, fees and interest. Figures are calculated at full precision and rounded for display. Your dealer may round taxes and fees slightly differently.

## Tips for buying within your budget

1. **Get preapproved first.** A rate from your bank or credit union gives you a figure to beat and keeps the conversation on price, not monthly payment.
2. **Negotiate the out-the-door price.** Dealers can hit any monthly payment by stretching the term. Agree the total price, then arrange the loan.
3. **Keep the term as short as you can afford.** The extra interest on longer loans adds up quickly, as the example shows.
4. **Check your trade-in value before you go.** Get quotes from more than one buyer so you know what a fair offer looks like.
5. **Budget for running costs.** Insurance, fuel and maintenance come on top of the loan payment. Leave room for them when you set your budget.
