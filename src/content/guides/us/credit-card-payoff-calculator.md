## How to use this calculator

The results update as you type.

- **Card balance:** what you owe today, from your latest statement or app.
- **Interest rate (APR):** the purchase APR shown on your statement. If your card has a promotional rate that's ending, use the rate that applies afterwards.
- **How do you want to pay it off?** Choose a fixed monthly payment to see how long it takes, or a target number of months to see the payment you need.
- **Monthly payment or months:** the amount you can pay each month, or how many months you want to take.

The results show your monthly payment, when you'll be debt-free, the total interest and the total you'll pay. If you're paying a fixed amount, you'll also see what an extra $50 a month would save. Below the calculator, the chart and schedule show how each year's payments split between interest and your balance.

## Example: a $5,000 balance at 21.99% APR

Say you owe $5,000 at 21.99% APR and stop using the card.

| Monthly payment | Time to pay off | Total interest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $100 | 137 months (11 years 5 months) | $8,656.83 |
| $200 | 34 months (2 years 10 months) | $1,748.69 |
| $250 | 26 months (2 years 2 months) | $1,284.93 |

Doubling the payment from $100 to $200 doesn't halve the time: it cuts it by three-quarters and saves $6,908.14 in interest. That's because in the first month, about $92 of interest is charged. At $100 a month, only around $8 of the first payment reduces the balance. At $200, about $108 does.

Going the other way, if you want to be debt-free in 24 months, you'd need $259.37 a month and would pay $1,224.79 in interest. Allowing 36 months lowers the payment to $190.93, but interest rises to $1,873.35.

## How it's calculated

Each month, interest is the balance × APR ÷ 12. The rest of your payment reduces the balance, and your last payment is only what's left.

**Fixed payment: months to pay off = −ln(1 − balance × r ÷ payment) ÷ ln(1 + r)**, rounded up to a whole month, where r is the monthly rate (APR ÷ 12 ÷ 100). For this to work, your payment must be more than the first month's interest. If it isn't, the balance never goes down, and the calculator tells you.

**Target date: payment = balance × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n)**, where n is the number of months. At 0% APR, it's simply the balance ÷ n.

Card issuers usually work out interest daily on your average balance, so your statement figures can differ a little from this estimate. The calculator assumes a steady rate, no fees and no new purchases. Figures are calculated at full precision and rounded for display.

## Tips to pay off your card faster

1. **Pay a fixed amount, not the minimum.** Minimum payments fall as your balance falls, which stretches the payoff out. Keeping your payment the same speeds things up.
2. **Add a little more each month.** As the example shows, an extra $50 on $200 clears the card 8 months sooner and saves $463.76.
3. **Stop adding new purchases.** Every new charge resets part of your progress. Use a debit card or cash while you pay the balance down.
4. **Pay the highest-rate card first if you have several.** Extra money goes furthest on the card that charges the most interest.
5. **Ask about a lower rate.** Some issuers will lower your APR if you ask, especially if you've paid on time. Even a few points makes a difference.
6. **Compare a consolidation loan.** A lower-rate personal loan can cut interest, but check the fee. Our debt consolidation calculator shows the break-even month.
