## How to use this calculator

The results update as you type.

- **Your home and loan:** the home price, your down payment in dollars, the interest rate and the loan term. The rate starts close to Freddie Mac's weekly average for a 30-year fixed mortgage, which was 7.24% on 29 October 2026. Use the rate on your Loan Estimate if you have one.
- **Taxes, insurance and fees:** property tax as a yearly percentage of the price, your homeowners insurance premium for a year, your PMI rate, and any HOA dues. If you know the yearly tax in dollars, divide it by the price and multiply by 100.
- **Pay it off faster:** an extra amount you pay each month, on top of your normal payment.

The results show your total monthly payment and each part of it, the loan amount, the total interest and PMI, your payoff date, and when PMI ends. Below the calculator, the chart shows the principal and interest you pay each year, and the amortization schedule gives the figures by year or by month.

## Example: a $400,000 home with 10% down

Say you buy a $400,000 home with $40,000 down (10%), and borrow $360,000 at 7.25% over 30 years. Property tax is 1.1% a year, insurance is $1,800 a year and PMI is 0.5% a year.

| Part of the payment | Each month |
| --- | --- |
| Principal and interest | $2,455.83 |
| Property tax | $366.67 |
| Homeowners insurance | $150.00 |
| PMI | $150.00 |
| **Total** | **$3,122.50** |

- **PMI** ends automatically after 118 payments, about 9 years and 10 months, when your balance is scheduled to reach 78% of the price. Your payment then drops to $2,972.50.
- **You could ask to cancel PMI** after 104 payments, once you owe 80% of the price.
- **Over 30 years** you'd pay $524,100.46 in interest and $17,700.00 in PMI.

Here's what changing one thing at a time does:

| Change | Monthly payment | Total interest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The example above | $3,122.50 | $524,100.46 |
| 20% down ($80,000), so no PMI | $2,699.63 | $465,867.07 |
| A 15-year term | $3,952.97 | $231,535.15 |
| A rate of 6.25% | $2,883.25 | $437,969.49 |
| $200 a month extra | $3,322.50 | $393,517.91 |

Paying $200 extra a month clears the loan 6 years 4 months early and saves $130,582.55 in interest. It also brings your balance to 80% of the price after 68 payments instead of 104.

## How it's calculated

**Principal and interest** each month:

**M = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n)**

where **P** is the loan amount (price − down payment), **r** the monthly rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and **n** the number of monthly payments. At 7.25%, r = 0.604%.

Each month, interest is the balance × r, and the rest of the payment reduces the balance. Early payments are mostly interest: in the example, the first payment is $2,175.00 of interest and $280.83 of principal. Any extra payment comes straight off the balance. The last payment is adjusted so the balance ends at exactly $0.

**The rest of the payment:**

- **Property tax** = price × yearly tax rate ÷ 12.
- **Homeowners insurance** = yearly premium ÷ 12.
- **PMI** = loan amount × PMI rate ÷ 12. It's charged only when you borrow more than 80% of the price, which the CFPB says lenders usually require on a conventional loan with less than 20% down.
- **HOA dues** are added as you enter them.

**When PMI ends.** Under the Homeowners Protection Act, as the CFPB explains it:

- You can ask your servicer in writing to cancel PMI once your balance reaches 80% of the home's original value. You need a good payment history, and you may have to show the home hasn't lost value.
- PMI must end automatically on the date your balance is scheduled to reach 78%.
- It must end the month after the midpoint of the loan at the latest, which is after 15 years on a 30-year loan.

The original value is generally the lower of the sale price and the appraised value; the calculator uses the price. The automatic date follows the original schedule, so extra payments bring forward the date you can ask to cancel, but not the automatic one.

The calculator assumes your rate, tax and insurance stay the same. Figures are calculated at full precision and rounded for display.

## Tips for your mortgage

1. **Compare more than one Loan Estimate.** Small rate differences add up. In the example, 6.25% instead of 7.25% saves $86,130.97 in interest.
2. **Weigh a bigger down payment.** Going from 10% to 20% down cuts the example payment by $422.87 a month and removes PMI. Balance that against keeping savings for repairs and emergencies.
3. **Note your PMI request date.** PMI ends on its own only at 78%. Once you reach 80%, write to your servicer to ask for it to be cancelled.
4. **Budget for the full payment.** Tax, insurance and PMI add $666.67 a month in the example. Property taxes and insurance can change from year to year, so your payment can too.
5. **Check before paying extra.** Make sure extra payments go to principal, and that your loan has no prepayment penalty.
6. **Look up the tax rate for the home itself.** Property tax rates vary by location, so check the county's figure or the listing rather than a national average.
