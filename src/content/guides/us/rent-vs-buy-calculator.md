## How to use this calculator

The results update as you type. Every field starts with an example value; change the ones you know.

- **The home:** the price, your down payment as a percentage, the mortgage rate and the term.
- **Buying and owning costs:** closing costs as a percentage of the price, your property tax rate, home insurance, maintenance (a share of the home's value each year) and any HOA fees.
- **Renting:** the monthly rent for a similar home, and how much you expect it to rise each year.
- **The future:** how long you'd stay, how fast you expect home prices to grow, what you'd earn on savings and investments, and the cost of selling.

The results show which choice leaves you better off after the years you'd stay, and by how much. They also show the year buying pulls ahead, your monthly mortgage payment and the cash you'd need up front. A table compares your net worth, the money paid out, the home value and the mortgage left. Below the calculator, the chart shows your net worth for 30 years either way, and the table gives every year's figures.

## Example: a $350,000 home or $2,000 a month in rent

Say you're choosing between buying a $350,000 home and renting a similar one for $2,000 a month. You'd put 20% down ($70,000) on a 30-year mortgage at 6.5%, which costs $1,769.79 a month. Closing costs are 3% ($10,500), so you'd need $80,500 up front.

In the first month, owning costs $2,532.29: the mortgage, plus property tax at 1.1%, $1,800 a year of insurance and 1% a year for maintenance. That's $532.29 more than the rent, so the renter invests that difference along with the $80,500, earning 5% a year. Home prices and rent both rise 3% a year, and selling costs 6%.

| 5 years | $119,290.43 | $133,905.30 | Rent by $14,614.86 |
| 10 years | $204,775.33 | $187,913.75 | Buy by $16,861.58 |
| 20 years | $459,139.21 | $309,246.40 | Buy by $149,892.81 |

Buying pulls ahead in year 8. After 10 years, the home is worth $470,370.73 and $237,373.15 is left on the mortgage.

The result is sensitive to your assumptions. Over 10 years:

- **Rent of $1,700** puts renting ahead by $35,599.68, and buying doesn't pull ahead until year 21.
- **Rent of $2,400** puts buying ahead by $86,809.94, from year 4.
- **A 7% investment return** puts renting ahead by $17,597.45.
- **Home prices growing 2% a year** put renting ahead by $18,758.05; at **4%**, buying is ahead by $55,947.89.

## How it's calculated

The calculator follows both choices month by month for 30 years.

**If you buy**, you pay the down payment and closing costs up front. Each month you pay the mortgage (monthly rate = rate ÷ 12, standard repayment), property tax and maintenance as a share of the home's value, insurance and any HOA fees. Your net worth is:

**home value − selling costs − mortgage left + any money you invested**

**If you rent**, you invest the money a buyer would have paid up front. In any month when owning costs more than rent, you invest the difference. When rent costs more, the buyer invests the difference instead. Your net worth is your investments.

Home prices grow each month at the equivalent of the yearly rate, and investments grow the same way at your chosen return. Rent rises once a year. Insurance and fees rise with home prices. **Buying pulls ahead** in the first year from which buying stays ahead through year 30.

The calculator leaves out income tax, the mortgage interest deduction and tax on investment gains, and assumes the rates you enter stay the same. Figures are calculated at full precision and rounded for display.

## Tips for deciding

1. **Be honest about how long you'll stay.** It's the biggest factor. Short stays rarely recover the cost of buying and selling.
2. **Compare like with like.** Use the rent for a home of the same size, area and quality as the one you'd buy.
3. **Use your real costs.** Your property tax bill, an insurance quote and your Loan Estimate will make the result much more accurate than the example figures.
4. **Try a few growth rates.** Nobody knows future home prices or investment returns. If buying wins only with optimistic growth, treat that as a warning.
5. **Count the renter's investing.** Renting comes out ahead only if you actually invest the money you'd have put into the home.
6. **Think beyond the numbers.** Stability, freedom to renovate, flexibility to move and the work of maintaining a home all matter too.
