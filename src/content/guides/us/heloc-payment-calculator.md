## How to use this calculator

The results update as you type.

- **Your HELOC:** the amount you'll borrow (the amount you draw, not your full credit limit), your interest rate, and the length of the draw and repayment periods from your agreement.
- **Payments in the draw period:** interest only, or principal and interest. You can also add an extra amount each month to pay the balance down sooner.
- **Test a rate rise:** most HELOC rates are variable. Enter a rise in percentage points to see what your repayment payment would be at the higher rate.
- **Your home (optional):** your home's value, your mortgage balance and the lender's maximum combined loan-to-value, to estimate your credit line.

The results show your payment in the draw period, your payment in the repayment period, how much it changes, the total interest and the interest paid in each period. Below the calculator, the chart shows the principal and interest you pay each year, and the table gives the full figures.

## Example: $50,000 at 8%

Say you draw $50,000 at 8%, with a 10-year draw period and a 20-year repayment period, paying interest only during the draw period.

- **Draw period:** $333.33 a month for 10 years. You pay $40,000 in interest, and you still owe the full $50,000.
- **Repayment period:** $418.22 a month for 20 years, an increase of $84.89 a month.
- **Total:** $90,372.81 of interest, and $140,372.81 paid altogether.

If the rate rises by 2 points to 10% before repayment starts, the repayment payment would be $482.51 a month.

The way you pay in the draw period makes a big difference:

| Draw period payments | Draw payment | Repayment payment | Total interest |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Interest only | $333.33 | $418.22 | $90,372.81 |
| Principal and interest | $366.88 | $366.88 | $82,077.62 |
| Interest only + $200 extra | $533.33 | $217.47 | $56,673.86 |

A shorter plan raises the payment jump. With a 5-year draw period and a 15-year repayment period, the payment goes from $333.33 to $477.83, an increase of $144.49 a month.

**Credit line:** with a $400,000 home, a $250,000 mortgage and a lender limit of 85% combined loan-to-value, the estimated credit line is $90,000. Borrowing $50,000 brings your combined loan-to-value to 75%.

## How it's calculated

**Interest each month** = balance × r, where r is the monthly rate: annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100. At 8%, r = 0.667%.

**Draw period:**

- **Interest only:** payment = balance × r, so the balance stays the same.
- **Principal and interest:** one level payment that would repay the balance over the draw and repayment periods together.
- Any extra payment comes straight off the balance.

**Repayment period:** the balance left is repaid in equal monthly payments:

**M = B × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n)**

where **B** is the balance when repayment starts and **n** the number of months in the repayment period. The last payment is adjusted so the balance ends at exactly 0.

**Credit line** = home value × maximum combined loan-to-value − mortgage balance.

The calculator assumes you draw the full amount at the start and the rate stays the same (apart from the rate-rise test). It doesn't include closing costs or annual fees. Figures are calculated at full precision and rounded for display.

## Tips for your HELOC

1. **Plan for the repayment period from day one.** Budget for the higher payment, not just the interest-only one.
2. **Pay down principal when you can.** Even a small extra amount each month during the draw period lowers your later payment and total interest.
3. **Test higher rates.** Most HELOC rates are variable. Make sure you could still afford the payment if the rate rose by a few points.
4. **Read the end-of-draw terms.** Some plans require the whole balance at the end of the draw period. Check your agreement so you aren't surprised by a balloon payment.
5. **Borrow only what you need.** You pay interest only on what you draw, so a large credit line doesn't have to mean a large balance.
6. **Remember your home is the security.** The CFPB advises taking out a HELOC only if you're confident you can make the payments, since you could lose your home if you fall behind.
