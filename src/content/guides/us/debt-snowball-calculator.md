  - q: What is the debt snowball method?
    a: You pay the minimum on every debt and put any extra money toward the smallest balance.     a: Yes. The plan keeps every minimum payment going and only moves the extra money. Missing a minimum payment can lead to late fees and a penalty rate, which would undo the progress you make.
  - q: Should I include my mortgage in the debt snowball?
    a: You can include any debt with a balance, rate and monthly payment. Many people leave a mortgage out because it's large, has a lower rate and runs for decades, and focus the snowball on cards and loans. Try it both ways to see the effect.

## How to use this calculator

The results update as you type.

- **Your debts:** for each one, enter a name (optional), the balance, the APR and the minimum monthly payment from your latest statement. Four rows are shown; use **Add another debt** for up to 10. Leave a balance empty to skip a row.
- **Extra each month:** how much you can pay on top of all your minimum payments. Enter 0 to see what rolling over payments alone does.
- **Payoff order:** the snowball targets the smallest balance first. The avalanche targets the highest APR first.

The results show the month you'll be debt-free, your total monthly payment, the total interest, and how much interest and time you save against paying only the minimums. A table compares the snowball, the avalanche and minimum payments only. Below the calculator, you'll see the payoff order with the month each debt is cleared, a yearly chart and a month-by-month schedule.

## Example: four debts and $200 extra a month

Say you owe four debts, $16,200 in total, and can add $200 a month to your minimum payments:

| Debt | Balance | APR | Minimum payment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Medical bill | $900 | 0% | $50 |
| Store card | $1,800 | 29.99% | $55 |
| Credit card | $4,500 | 24.99% | $120 |
| Car loan | $9,000 | 7.9% | $285 |

Your minimums add up to $510, so you pay $710 a month in total.

With the **snowball**, the medical bill goes first and is cleared in month 4. Its $50, plus the extra $200, then goes on the store card, which is gone by month 10. The growing payment clears the credit card in month 22 and the car loan in month 27. You pay $2,791.92 in interest in total.

With the **avalanche**, the store card goes first, because its rate is highest. It's cleared in month 8, the credit card in month 21 and the car loan in month 27. The medical bill is paid off by its own minimum payment in month 18. Interest comes to $2,580.47, which is $211.44 less than the snowball, for the same finish date.

Paying **only the minimums** would take 74 months (6 years 2 months) and cost $7,458.47 in interest. Compared with that, the snowball saves $4,666.55 and gets you debt-free 3 years 11 months sooner.

Even with no extra money, rolling each cleared payment into the next debt finishes in 43 months with $5,488.44 of interest. Raising the extra amount to $300 a month cuts the snowball to 23 months and $2,276.51 of interest.

## How it's calculated

Each month, the calculator works through four steps for every debt:

1. **Interest is added:** balance × APR ÷ 12. In month 1 of the example, that's $197.95 across all four debts.
2. **Every debt gets its minimum payment**, or the rest of its balance if that's less.
3. **The rest of your monthly budget goes to the target debt:** the smallest starting balance for the snowball, or the highest APR for the avalanche. If the target is cleared, anything left over goes straight to the next one.
4. **Your total payment stays the same.** When a debt is gone, its minimum payment becomes part of the money going to the next target.

**Minimums only** pays each debt its own minimum with no extra, and a cleared debt's payment isn't reused. If a minimum doesn't cover that debt's monthly interest, that debt is never paid off this way.

The calculator assumes fixed rates, fixed minimum payments, no new spending and no fees. Card issuers usually charge interest daily, so your statements can differ a little. Figures are calculated at full precision and rounded only for display.

## Tips to get out of debt faster

1. **List every debt first.** Include store cards, medical bills and personal loans, not just credit cards, so the plan covers everything you owe.
2. **Set every minimum on autopay.** A missed payment can bring a late fee and a penalty APR. Then pay the extra to your target debt by hand each month.
3. **Find a little more to add.** In the example, going from $200 to $300 extra a month finishes 4 months sooner and saves $515.41 of interest.
4. **Pause new borrowing while you pay down.** New charges add to the balances you're trying to clear and push your debt-free date back.
5. **Check the trade-off between snowball and avalanche.** If the avalanche saves only a small amount for your debts, the snowball's quick early wins may be worth it. If the gap is large, the avalanche keeps more money in your pocket.
6. **Ask for a lower rate.** A lower APR on your most expensive card helps either method. Before moving balances, check the transfer fee and the rate that applies afterward.
