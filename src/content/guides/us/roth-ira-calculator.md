## How to use this calculator

The results update as you type.

- **You:** your age, the age you plan to retire, your filing status and your modified adjusted gross income (MAGI). If your taxable compensation (wages and self-employment income) is different from your income, enter it too.
- **Your Roth IRA:** your balance today, how much you'd like to put in each year, and the yearly return you expect.

The results show your projected balance at retirement, your 2026 contribution limit, how much you'll put in this year, and the tax-free growth. If your income reduces or removes your limit, you'll see a note. Below the calculator, the chart shows your balance by age and the table gives each year's figures.

## Example: $7,500 a year from age 30

Say you're 30, single, with $75,000 of income and $5,000 already in a Roth IRA. Your income is below $153,000, so you can put in the full $7,500.

Contributing $7,500 a year until 65 at a 7% return, your Roth IRA could reach **$1,123,016**. You'd put in $262,500 (plus the $5,000 you started with), and $855,516 would be growth.

Starting age makes a big difference with the same $7,500 a year:

| Start at | Balance at 65 | Of which growth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 20 | $2,316,051 | $1,973,551 |
| 30 | $1,123,016 | $855,516 |
| 40 | $516,538 | $324,038 |

At a 5% return instead of 7%, starting at 30 gives $720,369.

**If your income is higher:** a single filer with $160,000 of MAGI can contribute $4,000 for 2026. From 50, the higher limit with catch-up raises that to $4,590. Contributing the most allowed each year from 30 would reach $639,150.

## How it's calculated

**Contribution limit:** $7,500 for 2026, or $8,600 from age 50 ($1,100 catch-up), but no more than your taxable compensation.

**Income phase-out:** the limit shrinks as your MAGI moves through your range: $153,000 to $168,000 (single or head of household), $242,000 to $252,000 (married filing jointly) or $0 to $10,000 (married filing separately, lived with spouse). Following IRS Publication 590-A Worksheet 2-2:

1. Share of range = (MAGI − start of range) ÷ width of range, rounded to three places.
2. Reduced limit = limit − limit × share, **rounded up to the next $10**, and at least **$200**.

For $160,000 single: (160,000 − 153,000) ÷ 15,000 = 0.467; $7,500 − $3,502.50 = $3,997.50, rounded up to $4,000.

**Growth:** your yearly contribution goes in monthly, and the balance grows at the monthly rate equal to your yearly return. The limits and your income are held at today's values, so the projection is in today's terms for contributions.

The calculator assumes you make no traditional IRA contributions, which would reduce your Roth limit. Figures are calculated at full precision and rounded for display.

## Tips for your Roth IRA

1. **Start early.** Time does most of the work. Starting at 20 instead of 30 more than doubles the balance in the example.
2. **Contribute steadily.** Setting up a monthly transfer makes it easier to reach the limit.
3. **Check your income each year.** If you're near the phase-out range, a raise or bonus can reduce your limit.
4. **Watch the deadline.** You can usually make contributions for a year until the tax filing deadline the following April.
5. **Keep it invested for the long term.** Qualified distributions are tax-free only when the requirements are met.
6. **Use your 401(k) match first if you have one.** Employer matching is extra money; then consider a Roth IRA for more tax-free growth.
