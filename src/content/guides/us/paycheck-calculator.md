## How to use this calculator

The results update as you type.

- **Your pay:** choose salary or hourly, enter your pay, and pick how often you're paid. Hourly pay is worked out as rate × hours × 52 weeks.
- **Your Form W-4:** your filing status, whether the Step 2 box is checked, and any amounts in Steps 3 and 4. Leave these at 0 if you didn't fill them in.
- **Deductions and state tax:** your pre-tax 401(k) or 403(b) percentage, pre-tax benefits per paycheck (such as health premiums), and an optional state and local tax rate.

The results show your take-home pay per paycheck and per year, with federal income tax, Social Security and Medicare, and pre-tax deductions. Below the calculator, the chart and table break down every paycheck and the whole year.

## Example: $65,000 a year, paid every two weeks

Say you earn $65,000 a year, are paid every two weeks (26 paychecks), file as single, and haven't checked the Step 2 box. You put 5% into a 401(k) and pay $75 per paycheck for health insurance before tax.

| Each paycheck | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Gross pay | $2,500.00 |
| 401(k), 5% | − $125.00 |
| Health premiums (pre-tax) | − $75.00 |
| Federal income tax | − $192.15 |
| Social Security | − $150.35 |
| Medicare | − $35.16 |
| Take-home pay | $1,922.33 |

Over the year, that's $49,980.68 of take-home pay, with $4,996.00 of federal income tax withheld.

How other choices change the paycheck:

- **Married filing jointly:** federal withholding falls to $108.31, so take-home pay is $2,006.18.
- **Head of household:** withholding is $150.92 and take-home pay is $1,963.56.
- **Step 2 box checked:** withholding rises to $336.19 and take-home pay falls to $1,778.30.
- **$2,200 in Step 3 for dependents:** withholding falls to $107.54.
- **A 5% state and local rate:** $115.00 comes off each paycheck, leaving $1,807.33.

## How it's calculated

Federal withholding follows the IRS percentage method for automated payroll in Publication 15-T for 2026 (Worksheet 1A):

1. **Annual wage:** taxable pay per paycheck (gross minus pre-tax 401(k) and benefits) × paychecks a year, plus Step 4(a) other income, minus Step 4(b) deductions. Unless the Step 2 box is checked, it also subtracts $12,900 if you're married filing jointly or $8,600 otherwise.
2. **Tentative withholding:** that amount runs through the 2026 tax brackets, which start after the standard deduction minus the same adjustment. For a single filer, withholding starts above $7,500. With the Step 2 box checked, the brackets and standard deduction are halved.
3. **Per paycheck:** the tentative amount ÷ paychecks, minus the Step 3 amount ÷ paychecks (not below zero), plus any Step 4(c) extra withholding.

In the example: $2,300 of taxable pay × 26 = $59,800, minus $8,600 = $51,200. Tax on that from the single schedule is $1,240 + 12% × ($51,200 − $19,900) = $4,996.00 a year, or $192.15 per paycheck.

**Social Security** is 6.2% of wages up to $184,500 for the year. **Medicare** is 1.45% of all wages, plus 0.9% on wages over $200,000. Pre-tax 401(k) contributions don't lower these taxes; Section 125 benefits such as health premiums do.

Figures are calculated at full precision and rounded for display. Your employer's payroll system may round each step to the cent or dollar, so payslips can differ by a few cents.

## Tips for a bigger paycheck, or a better refund

1. **Check your Form W-4 after a life change.** Marriage, a new baby, a second job or a raise can all change how much should be withheld.
2. **Use the IRS Tax Withholding Estimator once a year.** It compares your withholding with your likely tax for the year and tells you what to put on a new W-4.
3. **Remember pre-tax deductions cost less than they look.** In the example, $125 more into your 401(k) cuts take-home pay by $110, because it also lowers your income tax.
4. **Plan for the Social Security cap.** If you earn more than $184,500, your paychecks get bigger late in the year. Don't build your budget around those last paychecks.
5. **Account for state and local taxes.** Enter your state's rate for a more realistic take-home figure.
6. **Have a second job or side income?** Check the Step 2 box or add extra withholding in Step 4(c), or you may owe tax when you file.
