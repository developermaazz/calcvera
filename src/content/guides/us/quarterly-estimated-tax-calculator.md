## How to use this calculator

Enter your best estimate for the full 2026 tax year. The results update as you type.

- **Net self-employment income:** your business income minus business expenses, the profit you'd report on Schedule C.
- **Filing status:** single, married filing jointly, married filing separately or head of household. This sets your tax brackets, standard deduction and Additional Medicare Tax threshold.
- **W-2 wages:** pay from a job. Wages count towards the Social Security wage base, which can lower your self-employment tax.
- **Other taxable income:** interest, dividends, rental income and similar.
- **Deduction:** the standard deduction, or the total of your itemized deductions.
- **Federal tax withheld:** tax already taken from your paychecks this year. It reduces what you need to pay each quarter.

## Example: $60,000 of freelance profit

Say you're single, with $60,000 of net self-employment income and nothing else.

| Item | 2026 estimate |
| --- | --- |
| Self-employment tax | $8,477.73 |
| Federal income tax | $3,559.47 |
| Total federal tax | $12,037.20 |
| Each quarterly payment | $3,009.30 |

Your net earnings are $60,000 × 92.35% = $55,410, and self-employment tax is 15.3% of that. Half of it, $4,238.87, comes off your income, leaving adjusted gross income of $55,761.14. After the $16,100 standard deduction and a $7,932.23 qualified business income deduction, your taxable income is $31,728.91.

Notice that self-employment tax is more than twice your income tax here. Many new freelancers budget only for income tax and are caught out by this.

If you also earned $40,000 in wages with $3,000 withheld, and your side business made $30,000, your total federal tax would be $9,535.40. Withholding covers part of it, so each quarterly payment would be $1,633.85.

## How it's calculated

**Self-employment tax** (Schedule SE):

- Net earnings = profit × 92.35%. If that's under $400, no self-employment tax is due.
- Social Security part = 12.4% × net earnings, up to the $184,500 wage base minus any W-2 wages.
- Medicare part = 2.9% × all net earnings.

**Additional Medicare Tax** = 0.9% of net earnings above your threshold ($200,000 single or head of household, $250,000 married filing jointly, $125,000 married filing separately), after your wages use up part of the threshold.

**Income tax:**

1. Adjusted gross income = all income − half of self-employment tax.
2. Subtract your standard or itemized deduction.
3. Subtract the qualified business income deduction: 20% of profit after the deductible part of self-employment tax, capped at 20% of taxable income. Above the threshold for your filing status, the calculator phases it down to zero across the phase-in range, assuming no employees or business property. Taxpayers with at least $1,000 of qualified business income get a minimum $400 deduction.
4. Apply the 2026 federal tax rates for your filing status.

**Quarterly payment** = (total tax − tax withheld) ÷ 4.

The estimate doesn't include tax credits, the self-employed health insurance deduction, retirement contributions or state tax.

## Tips for paying estimated tax

1. **Set aside money from every payment you receive.** Moving a fixed share of each invoice into a separate account makes quarterly payments easier.
2. **Use the safe harbor if your income is uneven.** Paying 100% of last year's tax (110% if your 2025 AGI was over $150,000) in four equal parts generally avoids a penalty, even if this year turns out higher.
3. **Re-run the numbers mid-year.** If your income changes, adjust your remaining payments rather than waiting for April.
4. **Consider retirement contributions.** Contributions to a SEP-IRA or solo 401(k) can lower your income tax. They aren't included in this estimate.
5. **Don't forget your state.** Your state may expect its own quarterly payments on different dates.
