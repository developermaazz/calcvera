## How to use this calculator

Use this calculator for a loan you already have. The results update as you type.

- **Loan balance left:** what you owe today. Your lender's app or statement often shows a payoff amount.
- **Interest rate (APR):** the APR on your loan agreement.
- **Current monthly payment:** your required payment.
- **Extra payment each month:** any amount you plan to add on top.
- **One-time extra payment now:** a lump sum, such as a bonus or tax refund, paid straight away.
- **Prepayment penalty:** a fee for paying early, if your loan agreement has one.

The results compare your current plan with paying extra: monthly payment, time left, payoff date and interest. The headline shows what you'd save after any penalty. Below, the chart shows the total you have left to pay on each plan, and the schedule lists every payment when paying extra.

## Example: $12,000 left at 11.5% APR

Say you owe $12,000 at 11.5% APR and pay $350 a month. On your current plan, you'll be debt-free in 42 months (3 years 6 months) and pay $2,618.25 in interest.

| Plan | Time left | Interest | Saving |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Current payment, $350 | 42 months | $2,618.25 | none |
| $50 extra a month | 36 months | $2,216.70 | $401.55 |
| $100 extra a month | 31 months | $1,924.08 | $694.17 |
| $200 extra a month | 25 months | $1,527.44 | $1,090.81 |
| $2,000 lump sum now | 34 months | $1,741.19 | $877.06 |
| $2,000 now and $100 a month | 26 months | $1,299.15 | $1,319.10 |

Notice the lump sum. Paying $2,000 once saves more interest than paying $100 extra every month until the loan is gone, even though those monthly extras add up to more than $2,000. Money that reduces the balance today avoids interest for every remaining month.

If your loan had a $250 prepayment penalty, the $100-a-month plan would still save you $444.17. A penalty above $694.17 would wipe out the saving, and the calculator would warn you.

## How it's calculated

Each month, interest is the balance × APR ÷ 12. The rest of your payment reduces the balance, and the last payment is only what's left.

1. **Current plan:** your current payment, month by month, until the balance reaches zero.
2. **Paying extra:** the balance minus any one-time payment, repaid at your current payment plus the monthly extra.
3. **You save = interest on the current plan − interest paying extra − prepayment penalty.**

The calculator assumes extra money goes straight to principal, the rate doesn't change, and you make every payment on time. Your payment must be more than one month's interest, or the balance would never go down. Figures are calculated at full precision and rounded for display, so they may differ by a few cents from your lender's statements.

## Tips for paying off a loan early

1. **Check for a prepayment penalty first.** Your loan disclosures must say whether one may apply. Enter it to see the real saving.
2. **Keep an emergency fund.** Don't use your last savings to pay down a loan. If something goes wrong, you may end up borrowing again at a higher rate.
3. **Pay off higher-rate debt first.** If you also carry a credit card balance, extra money usually saves more there.
4. **Use windfalls.** A tax refund or bonus paid as a lump sum early in the loan saves the most interest.
5. **Ask for extra to go to principal.** Confirm how your lender applies extra payments, and check your next statement to see the balance fall.
6. **Round up your payment.** Even rounding $350 up to $400 clears this example loan 6 months sooner.
