## How to use this calculator

The results update as you type.

- **Starting amount:** what you're putting in now. Enter 0 if you're starting from nothing.
- **Monthly deposit:** how much you'll add each month. Leave it at 0 for a one-off lump sum.
- **Annual interest rate:** the stated yearly rate. If your bank only shows the APY, enter that and choose "Annually".
- **Interest compounds:** how often interest is added: daily, monthly, quarterly, twice a year or annually. Savings accounts often compound daily or monthly.
- **How long:** the number of years you'll leave the money to grow.
- **Deposits are made at the:** start or end of each month. Deposits at the start earn one extra month of interest.

The results show your balance at the end, how much you put in, the interest earned and the APY. Below the calculator, the chart shows your balance at the end of each year, split into deposits and interest, and the table gives the figures for every year.

## Example: $10,000 plus $200 a month at 5%

Say you start with $10,000, add $200 at the end of each month, and earn 5% compounded monthly for 20 years.

| After | Balance | Put in | Interest |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10 years | $47,526.55 | $34,000.00 | $13,526.55 |
| 20 years | $109,333.14 | $58,000.00 | $51,333.14 |
| 30 years | $211,129.17 | $82,000.00 | $129,129.17 |

After 20 years, interest makes up 47% of the balance. The second 10 years earn almost three times as much interest as the first 10, because interest is now being earned on a much bigger balance.

Small changes add up:

- **Rate:** at 4% the 20-year balance is $95,580.75; at 6% it's $125,510.22.
- **Deposit:** $400 a month instead of $200 gives $191,539.87.
- **Timing:** depositing at the start of each month adds $342.52.
- **Starting later:** saving for 15 years instead of 20 gives $74,594.83, about $34,700 less.

## How it's calculated

Your monthly deposits and the bank's compounding don't always line up, so the calculator first finds the monthly rate that gives exactly the same yearly growth:

**i = (1 + r ÷ n)^(n ÷ 12) − 1**

- **r** is the annual rate as a decimal (5% = 0.05).
- **n** is how many times a year interest compounds: 365 for daily, 12 for monthly, 4 for quarterly, 2 for twice a year and 1 for annually.

Each month, the balance grows by i and your deposit is added, at the start or the end of the month. A lump sum on its own grows to **P × (1 + r ÷ n)^(n × t)** after t years, the standard compound interest formula.

The **APY** is **(1 + r ÷ n)^n − 1**: the yearly growth after compounding. Banks must show the APY on savings accounts, which makes it the fairest way to compare them.

The calculator assumes the rate stays the same and leaves out tax, fees and inflation. Figures are calculated at full precision and rounded only for display.

## Tips to make compound interest work harder

1. **Start as early as you can.** Time does most of the work. In the example, the last 10 years add more interest than the first 10.
2. **Compare accounts by APY.** It includes compounding, so two accounts with different compounding can be compared directly.
3. **Set up an automatic transfer.** A fixed monthly deposit on payday is easier to keep up than saving what's left at the end of the month.
4. **Leave the interest in.** Withdrawing interest stops it from earning more interest.
5. **Check whether the rate is fixed.** Many savings rates can change at any time, and introductory rates often drop after a few months.
6. **Remember tax and inflation.** Interest is usually taxable, and rising prices reduce what your balance will buy. Your real growth is lower than the figure shown.
