## How to use this calculator

The calculator compares two paths: paying your debts as you are now, or replacing them with one fixed-rate loan.

- **Your current debts:** for each debt, enter the balance, APR and the monthly payment you actually make. Three rows are shown; use "Add another debt" for up to 10. Leave a balance empty to skip a row.
- **New loan APR:** the rate on the consolidation loan you've been offered.
- **New loan term:** how long the loan lasts.
- **Origination fee:** the upfront fee as a percentage of the loan, often taken out of the money you receive.

The results show your monthly payment, payoff time and interest for both paths, the fee, the total saving or extra cost, and the break-even month.

## Example: three debts and a 36-month loan

Say you have:

| Debt | Balance | APR | Monthly payment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Credit card 1 | $6,000 | 22.99% | $200 |
| Credit card 2 | $3,500 | 27.49% | $120 |
| Personal loan | $8,000 | 11.5% | $250 |

At these payments, you'd be debt-free in 4 years 1 month and pay $6,956.53 in interest, paying $570 a month in total.

Now compare a consolidation loan at 9.99% over 36 months with a 5% origination fee. To clear $17,500, you borrow $18,421.05, so the fee is $921.05. Your new payment is $594.31 a month and total interest is $2,974.07.

Consolidating saves $3,061.41 in interest and fees, and you're debt-free a year sooner. The interest you avoid covers the fee by month 8.

Two changes show how sensitive this is:

- **60-month term:** the payment drops to $391.30, but the saving shrinks to $978.40.
- **15.99% APR:** the saving falls to $1,145.14, and the fee takes until month 24 to pay for itself.

## How it's calculated

**Current debts.** Each debt is paid at its own monthly payment until it's gone. Each month, interest is the balance × APR ÷ 12, and the rest of the payment reduces the balance. Payments aren't moved to another debt when one is paid off. If a payment doesn't cover a debt's monthly interest, that debt never gets paid off, and the calculator tells you.

**Loan amount = total balances ÷ (1 − fee %)**

**New monthly payment = loan × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n)**, where r is the monthly rate (APR ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n is the number of months.

**Savings = current interest − (new loan interest + fee)**

**Break-even** is the first month when cumulative interest on your current debts, minus cumulative interest on the new loan, is at least the fee.

All figures are calculated at full precision and rounded for display.

## Tips for consolidating debt

1. **Compare the APR, not just the payment.** A lower payment from a longer term can hide a higher total cost.
2. **Ask whether the fee comes out of the loan.** If it does, borrow enough to clear every balance in full.
3. **Pick the shortest term you can afford.** As the example shows, the term matters as much as the rate.
4. **Check for prepayment penalties.** If you plan to pay the loan off early, make sure you won't be charged for it.
5. **Have a plan for the cleared cards.** Consolidation only saves money if the old balances stay at zero.
