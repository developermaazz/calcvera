  - q: How much is zakat?
    a: Zakat is 2.5% of your net zakatable wealth, if it reaches the nisab and you've held it for a full lunar year. For example, Rs 600,000 of savings gives zakat of Rs 15,000.
  - q: What is the nisab in Pakistan?
    a: Under the Zakat and Ushr Ordinance, 1980, the nisab is the value of 612.32 grams of silver, as notified for each Zakat year, or 87.48 grams of gold if your zakatable assets are only gold. The notified nisab for Zakat year 1446–1447 AH was Rs 503,529.
  - q: Which assets count for zakat?
    a: Cash, bank and savings balances, gold and silver, shares and mutual funds, goods held for trade, and money owed to you that you expect to receive. Your home, car, household goods and personal items generally don't count. Scholars differ on some assets, so ask a scholar if you're unsure.
  - q: Do I subtract debts?
    a: Debts and bills due now are usually subtracted before working out zakat. Opinions differ on long-term debts such as a home loan; many scholars subtract only the instalments due now.
  - q: Do banks deduct zakat automatically?
    a: Banks deduct zakat at 2.5% on the first day of Ramadan from savings and profit-and-loss sharing accounts with a balance at or above the notified nisab. You can file a declaration of exemption if you qualify.
  - q: Should I use the gold or silver nisab?
    a: The Ordinance uses the silver nisab, and the gold nisab only when all your zakatable assets are gold. Because silver is cheaper, the silver nisab is lower, so more people reach it.

## How to use this calculator

The results update as you type.

- **Cash and savings:** cash at home, bank and savings balances, investments and trade goods at today's value, and money owed to you.
- **Gold and silver:** how many grams you own and today's price per 10 grams. One tola is about 11.66 grams.
- **Debts and nisab:** debts and bills due now, and which nisab to use: the official notified amount, the silver nisab at today's price, or the gold nisab if you own only gold.

The results show the zakat due, your net zakatable assets, the nisab and whether zakat is due. The breakdown lists each asset.

## Example: savings and some gold

Say you have Rs 200,000 in cash, Rs 800,000 in the bank, Rs 300,000 in shares and mutual funds, and 23.3 grams of gold (about 2 tola). You owe Rs 150,000 that's due now. For the example, gold is priced at Rs 400,000 per 10 grams; use today's rate for your own calculation.

- **Gold:** 23.3 × Rs 400,000 ÷ 10 = Rs 932,000.
- **Total assets:** Rs 2,232,000.
- **Net zakatable assets:** Rs 2,232,000 − Rs 150,000 = Rs 2,082,000.
- **Nisab:** Rs 503,529 (notified for Zakat year 1446–1447 AH), so zakat is due.
- **Zakat:** 2.5% × Rs 2,082,000 = **Rs 52,050**.

**Near the nisab:** with Rs 400,000 in the bank and nothing else, you're below the notified nisab of Rs 503,529. But if silver is priced at Rs 5,000 per 10 grams (an example), the silver nisab is 612.32 × Rs 500 = Rs 306,160, and zakat of Rs 10,000 would be due. That's why the nisab you use matters.

## How it's calculated

**Net zakatable assets** = cash + bank balances + gold + silver + investments and trade goods + money owed to you − debts due now.

Gold and silver are valued at the price you enter: grams × price per 10 grams ÷ 10.

**Nisab**, under the Zakat and Ushr Ordinance, 1980:

- the value of **612.32 grams of silver**, as notified by the Administrator-General for each Zakat year; or
- **87.48 grams of gold**, where your zakatable assets are only gold.

The notified nisab for Zakat year 1446–1447 AH was **Rs 503,529**. You can also use today's silver price for your own calculation.

**Zakat** = 2.5% of net zakatable assets, if they reach the nisab. Zakat is due on wealth held for a full lunar year.

This is a general guide, not a religious ruling. Scholars differ on some assets, such as jewellery in personal use, pension funds and long-term debts. Figures are calculated at full precision and rounded for display.

## Tips for your zakat

1. **Pick a zakat date.** Calculate on the same date each lunar year, often in Ramadan.
2. **Use today's prices.** Gold, silver and share values change, so check them on your zakat date.
3. **Check bank deductions.** If your bank already deducted zakat from a savings account, take that into account.
4. **Count business stock at sale value.** Goods held for sale are usually valued at what they'd sell for.
5. **Keep records.** Note your assets, debts and the prices you used each year.
6. **Ask a scholar if unsure.** Especially for jewellery, pensions, property held for sale or complex investments.
