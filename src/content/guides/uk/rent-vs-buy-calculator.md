## How to use this calculator

Change any box and the answer updates. Each one starts with an example you can replace.

- **The home:** price, deposit (as a percentage), mortgage rate and term.
- **Buying and owning costs:** first-time buyer or moving home, other buying costs, buildings insurance, maintenance as a yearly share of the value, and any service charge and ground rent.
- **Renting:** the rent for a home like the one you'd buy, and how quickly you think it will rise.
- **The future:** how long you'd stay, house price growth, the return on savings and investments, and the cost of selling.

You'll see which choice leaves you better off and by how much, when buying starts to win, the mortgage payment and the cash you'd need. The chart and table below cover every year up to 30.

The calculator starts with prices rising 2% a year and rents 3%. The latest ONS figures are 1.2% for UK house prices, 12 months to September 2026, and 3.8% for rents, to October 2026, both provisional. Try both, because prices won't grow at last year's rate forever.

## Example: £300,000 home or £1,300 a month rent

You're a first-time buyer with a 15% deposit (£45,000) and £2,500 of other buying costs. No Stamp Duty is due, so you'd need £47,500. A 25-year mortgage at 4.5% costs £1,417.37 a month. Add £350 a year of insurance and 1% maintenance, and the first month of owning costs £1,696.54, which is £396.54 more than the rent. A renter invests that difference and the £47,500, earning 4% a year. Selling costs 2%.

With the ONS rates (prices +1.2%, rents +3.8%), buying pulls ahead in year 3. After 10 years, the buyer's net worth is £147,421.11 and the renter's is £98,625.27, so buying leads by **£48,795.84**. The lead is £10,086.78 at 5 years and £222,343.21 at 20. With the calculator's starting figures (prices 2%, rents 3%), buying wins from year 2 and by £66,857.80 at 10 years. Here's what changes the 10-year answer, starting from the ONS rates:

| Change | Buying's lead at 10 years | Buying pulls ahead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mortgage rate 5.5% | £20,635.36 | Year 7 |
| Mortgage rate 6.5% | Renting by £8,234.04 | Year 12 |
| Rent £1,100 instead of £1,300 | £14,313.99 | Year 7 |
| Service charge £200 a month | £17,939.69 | Year 7 |
| Investments earn 6% | £29,798.71 | Year 5 |
| Moving home, £5,000 Stamp Duty | £41,394.62 | Year 5 |

Of these changes, the mortgage rate moves the answer most: two points more turns buying's £48,795.84 lead into an £8,234.04 lead for renting.

## How it's calculated

The calculator follows both choices month by month for 30 years.

**Buying:** you pay the deposit, Stamp Duty and other costs at the start, then the mortgage (rate ÷ 12, standard repayment), maintenance, insurance and any service charge. Your net worth is **home value − selling costs − mortgage left + money you invested**.

**Renting:** you invest the up-front cash, then the difference in any month when owning costs more than rent. When rent is dearer, the buyer invests the difference. Your net worth is the investments.

Home prices and returns grow at monthly rates that match the yearly ones, rent rises once a year, and insurance and service charges follow house prices. Council Tax is left out because you pay it either way. Tax on investment gains and changes in your mortgage rate aren't included. "Pulls ahead" means the first year from which buying stays ahead until year 30. Figures are rounded only when shown.

## Tips for UK decisions

1. **Stress test the mortgage rate.** A fixed deal ends after a few years, so run the figures at a higher rate before you commit.
2. **Compare service charges closely.** A flat's monthly charge can shift the answer by years.
3. **Remember the £500,000 line.** First-time buyer relief stops above it, so a small price change can cost thousands.
4. **Use a Lifetime ISA if you qualify.** It can add a 25% bonus to a first deposit. Our [UK savings goal calculator](/uk/savings-goal-calculator/) explains the rules.
5. **Budget for a tenancy deposit if you rent.** It's cash you need now, even though you should get it back.
6. **Be honest about moving.** A short stay rarely covers the costs of buying and selling.
