## How to use this calculator

The results update as you type.

- **Already in your ISA:** your current ISA balance. It doesn't use this year's allowance.
- **Pay in each month:** how much you'll add each month.
- **Interest rate (AER):** the rate your cash ISA pays, or the yearly growth you expect for a stocks and shares ISA.
- **How long:** the number of years you'll save.
- **Your Income Tax band:** used to work out the tax on the same savings outside an ISA.

The results show your ISA balance at the end, how much of the yearly allowance you use, the tax you'd pay in a normal savings account, and how much better off you are in an ISA. Below the calculator, the chart shows your balance each year and the table compares the ISA with a taxable account.

## Example: £300 a month for 10 years at 4%

Say you have £5,000 in a cash ISA and add £300 a month at 4% AER for 10 years. That's £3,600 a year, well within the £20,000 allowance.

Your ISA grows to **£51,410.00**: £41,000 paid in and £10,410.00 of interest, all tax-free. In year 10 alone, the interest is £1,901.86.

The same savings in a normal savings account:

| Tax band | Tax paid over 10 years | Balance after tax | Better off in an ISA |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Basic rate | £489.78 | £50,896.11 | £513.89 |
| Higher rate | £2,209.57 | £49,000.05 | £2,409.95 |
| Additional rate | £4,414.24 | £46,395.18 | £5,014.82 |

The ISA's lead grows over time. Over 20 years, a basic-rate taxpayer saving the same amount would be £5,795.50 better off. Someone using almost the full allowance (£1,666 a month) for 20 years as a higher-rate taxpayer would be £92,922.05 better off.

## How it's calculated

**Growth:** the calculator turns the AER into a monthly rate, i = (1 + AER)^(1/12) − 1, and adds your deposit at the end of each month. At 4% AER, i = 0.327% a month.

**ISA allowance:** your deposits in each year (monthly amount × 12) are checked against the £20,000 limit for 2026 to 2027.

**Outside an ISA:** each tax year, interest over your Personal Savings Allowance (£1,000 basic rate, £500 higher rate, £0 additional rate) is taxed at your usual rate: 20%, 40% or 45%. The tax is taken off the balance at the end of the year, so the taxable account also loses the interest that money would have earned. That's why the ISA advantage is more than the tax itself.

The comparison leaves out the starting rate for savings, which helps people with low other income, and assumes your rate and tax band stay the same. Figures are calculated at full precision and rounded for display.

## Tips for your ISA

1. **Use your allowance each year if you can.** Unused allowance doesn't carry over to the next tax year.
2. **Shop around for rates.** Cash ISA rates vary between providers. If you move an existing ISA, use the provider's transfer service rather than withdrawing, so it keeps its tax-free status.
3. **Match the ISA to your timeline.** Cash suits money you'll need within a few years. A stocks and shares ISA may suit longer goals, but its value can fall.
4. **Check fixed-rate terms.** Fixed-rate cash ISAs often charge a penalty for withdrawing early.
5. **Think about your tax band later on.** Even if your interest is tax-free now, a pay rise or higher rates could push it over your Personal Savings Allowance.
6. **Consider pensions too.** For retirement, pension contributions get tax relief going in, while ISAs give tax-free access at any time.
