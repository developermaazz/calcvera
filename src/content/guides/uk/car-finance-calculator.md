## How to use this calculator

The results update as you type.

- **Car price:** the cash price of the car.
- **Deposit:** the cash you'll put down, plus the value of any part-exchange.
- **Agreement length:** how many months you'll make payments.
- **APR:** the rate on your finance quote.
- **Optional final payment (PCP):** the amount due at the end if you want to keep the car. Enter 0 for hire purchase.
- **Option to purchase fee:** a small fee some lenders charge with the final payment.

The results show your monthly payment, the amount borrowed, the final payment, the total amount payable, and how much of that is interest and fees. For PCP, you'll also see the monthly payment as hire purchase, and the point at which you could hand the car back.

## Example: a £25,000 car on PCP

Say you're buying a £25,000 car with a £3,000 deposit, over 48 months at 9.9% APR, with a £9,000 optional final payment. Both options include a £10 option-to-purchase fee.

| Item | PCP | Hire purchase |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Amount borrowed | £22,000.00 | £22,000.00 |
| Monthly payment | £403.34 | £556.92 |
| Final payment | £9,000.00 | none |
| Total amount payable | £31,370.30 | £29,742.20 |
| Interest and fees | £6,370.30 | £4,742.20 |

PCP saves you £153.58 a month, but if you keep the car it costs £1,628.10 more overall. That's because you pay interest on the £9,000 final payment for all 48 months, while hire purchase pays it down from the start.

PCP can still suit you if you plan to hand the car back or change it at the end, as long as you stay within the mileage limit and look after the car.

Under the Consumer Credit Act, you can usually end the agreement and return the car once you've paid half the total price. On the PCP example, that's £15,685.15, including your deposit.

## How it's calculated

1. **Amount borrowed** = car price − deposit.
2. **Monthly payment = (amount borrowed − final payment ÷ (1 + r)^n) × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n)**, where r is the monthly rate (APR ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n is the number of months. The final payment is paid at the end, so it's discounted back to today. With no final payment, this is a standard hire purchase payment.
3. **Total amount payable** = deposit + monthly payments + final payment + option fee.
4. **Interest and fees** = total amount payable − car price.

Lenders work from the APR as an annual compound rate, so your quote may differ by a few pence a month. The calculator doesn't include mileage or damage charges at the end of a PCP agreement.

## Tips for getting car finance

1. **Compare the total amount payable, not just the monthly payment.** A lower payment can hide a higher total cost.
2. **Decide what you'll do at the end.** If you want to keep the car, compare PCP with hire purchase or a personal loan.
3. **Set a realistic mileage limit.** Excess mileage charges can add up quickly on PCP.
4. **Get quotes from more than one lender.** Dealer finance isn't the only option; banks and other lenders offer car finance and personal loans.
5. **Know your right to hand the car back.** Once you've paid half the total price, you can usually end a hire purchase or PCP agreement.
