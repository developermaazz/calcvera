## How to use this calculator

The results update as you type.

- **Your mortgage:** what you still owe, your interest rate and the time left. Decimals work for the term: 22.5 is 22 years 6 months.
- **Overpay each month:** a regular amount on top of your normal payment.
- **One-off lump sum:** an amount you'll pay once, and the month you'll pay it (1 is with your next payment).
- **What overpayments should do:** pay the mortgage off sooner, or keep the same end date and lower your monthly payment.
- **Yearly overpayment allowance:** the most your lender lets you overpay each year without an early repayment charge, as a percentage of your balance.

The results show the interest you save, your mortgage-free date or new payment, and the total interest compared with not overpaying. Below the calculator, the chart shows your balance each year with and without overpayments, and the table gives the figures.

## Example: £200,000 at 4.5% with 25 years left

Say you owe £200,000 at 4.5% with 25 years to go. Your payment is £1,111.66 a month, and you'll pay £133,499.49 in interest over the full term.

| Overpayment | Mortgage-free in | Interest saved |
| --- | --- | --- |
| £100 a month | 21 years 6 months | £21,141.79 |
| £200 a month | 18 years 11 months | £36,280.39 |
| £500 a month | 14 years | £63,886.72 |

A one-off **£10,000 lump sum in month 12** saves £18,072.49 and finishes 2 years 1 month early. If you ask your lender to lower your payment instead, it falls to £1,054.82 a month and you save £6,370.64.

Watch the allowance: overpaying £500 a month goes over a 10% yearly allowance in years 12 to 14, because the balance has fallen by then. The calculator flags any year where that happens.

## How it's calculated

Your monthly payment comes from the standard repayment mortgage formula:

**M = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n)**

- **P** is what you owe, **n** the months left and **r** the monthly rate: annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100. At 4.5%, r = 0.375%.
- Each month, interest = balance × r. Your payment covers that interest and repays some of the balance.
- Overpayments come straight off the balance after your normal payment, so less interest builds up from then on.
- **Pay off sooner** keeps the payment the same, so the mortgage ends early. **Lower payments** works the payment out again each month over the time left on your original term.

The calculator assumes your rate stays the same. If you're on a fixed deal that ends soon, try your expected new rate too. Figures are calculated at full precision and rounded for display.

## Tips for overpaying your mortgage

1. **Check your allowance first.** Find the overpayment limit and any early repayment charges in your mortgage offer or illustration, or ask your lender.
2. **Keep an emergency fund.** Money paid into your mortgage is hard to get back if you need it.
3. **Tell your lender what you want.** Ask whether overpayments should shorten your term or lower your payment.
4. **Start early.** Overpayments early in the mortgage save the most, because the balance and the interest on it are highest.
5. **Compare with your savings rate.** If savings or an ISA pay more than your mortgage rate after tax, overpaying may not be the best use of the money.
6. **Use the end of a fixed deal.** Switching deals is often a good time to pay a larger lump sum, as early repayment charges usually stop when the fixed period ends.
