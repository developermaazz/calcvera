## How to use this calculator

The results update as you type.

- **Card balance:** what you owe today, from your latest statement or app.
- **Interest rate:** the annual purchase rate on your statement. If you have a 0% offer that's ending, use the rate that applies afterwards.
- **How do you want to pay it off?** Choose a fixed monthly payment to see how long it takes, or a target number of months to see the payment you need.
- **Monthly payment or months:** the amount you can pay each month, or how many months you want to take.

The results show your monthly payment, when you'll be debt-free, the total interest and the total you'll repay. If you pay a fixed amount, you'll also see what an extra £50 a month would save. Below the calculator, the chart and schedule show how each year's payments split between interest and your balance.

## Example: a £3,000 balance at 24.9%

Say you owe £3,000 at 24.9% and stop using the card.

| Monthly payment | Time to pay off | Total interest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| £70 | 108 months (9 years) | £4,501.25 |
| £150 | 27 months (2 years 3 months) | £915.95 |
| £200 | 19 months (1 year 7 months) | £631.43 |

At £70 a month, you'd pay back £4,501.25 in interest, more than the £3,000 you owe. Most of each early payment goes on interest: in the first month, £62.25 of interest is charged, so only £7.75 of a £70 payment reduces the balance.

Under the FCA rule, the first minimum payment must cover at least the interest plus 1% of the balance, or £92.25 here. If you kept paying that same £92.25 every month instead of letting it fall, you'd clear the card in 55 months and pay £2,045.81 in interest. Real minimum payments fall as the balance falls, which takes longer still.

If you'd rather pick a date, clearing the card in 24 months needs £159.96 a month and costs £839.14 in interest. In 12 months, it's £284.99 a month and £419.84 in interest.

## How it's calculated

Each month, interest is the balance × annual rate ÷ 12. The rest of your payment reduces the balance, and your last payment is only what's left.

**Fixed payment: months to pay off = −ln(1 − balance × r ÷ payment) ÷ ln(1 + r)**, rounded up to a whole month, where r is the monthly rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100). Your payment must be more than the first month's interest. If it isn't, the balance never goes down, and the calculator tells you.

**Target date: payment = balance × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n)**, where n is the number of months. At 0%, it's the balance ÷ n.

Card providers usually charge interest daily, so your statement figures can differ a little from this estimate. The calculator assumes a steady rate, no fees and no new spending. Figures are calculated at full precision and rounded for display.

## Tips to clear your card faster

1. **Set a fixed Direct Debit above the minimum.** A fixed amount stays the same as your balance falls, so more of it goes on the balance over time.
2. **Add a little more each month.** In the example, raising £150 to £200 clears the card 8 months sooner and saves £284.52.
3. **Stop using the card while you pay it off.** New spending adds to the balance you're trying to clear.
4. **Pay the highest-rate card first if you have several.** Extra money saves the most there.
5. **Look at balance transfer deals carefully.** Check the transfer fee, how long the 0% lasts and what rate applies after.
6. **Get free help if you're struggling.** If your payments mostly cover interest, contact your card provider or a free debt advice service such as MoneyHelper, StepChange or Citizens Advice.
