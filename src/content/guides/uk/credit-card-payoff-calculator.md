## How to use this calculator

The result changes as you edit the boxes.

- **Card balance:** what you owe now, from your statement or app.
- **Interest rate (purchase rate):** your statement's annual rate. If a 0% offer ends soon, use the rate that follows it.
- **How do you want to pay it off?** Enter a fixed monthly payment to see how long it takes, or pick a number of months to see the payment you need.

You'll get the time, the date, the total interest and the total paid. With a fixed payment, you'll also see what an extra £50 a month would do. The chart and schedule show how much of each payment goes on interest.

Add up any other cards and loans in the [UK debt snowball calculator](/uk/debt-snowball-calculator/), which works out the order to clear them in.

## Example: £3,000 at 24.9%

You owe £3,000 at 24.9% and stop using the card. In the first month, £62.25 of interest is added.

| Monthly payment | Cleared in | Total interest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| £70 | 9 years | £4,501.25 |
| £100 | 4 years | £1,743.66 |
| £150 | 2 years 3 months | £915.95 |
| £200 | 1 year 7 months | £631.43 |

Doubling the payment from £100 to £200 cuts the time from 48 months to 19 and saves £1,112.24. Going the other way, £70 a month costs more in interest than the £3,000 you borrowed.

### Would this card be in persistent debt?

The FCA test compares the money that went on interest with the money that reduced your balance over 18 months. For this card, with no new spending:

- **£70 a month:** £1,092.95 of interest and £167.05 off the balance, so yes.
- **£100 a month:** £986.30 of interest and £813.70 off the balance, so yes.
- **£107 a month:** £961.41 of interest and £964.59 off the balance, so no, just.

That makes £107 the lowest whole-pound payment that keeps this card out of persistent debt. The lender's later options should usually clear the balance in 3 to 4 years: £119.12 a month does it in 36 months, with £1,288.35 of interest, and £99.30 in 48 months, with £1,766.60.

## How it's calculated

Each month, the balance earns interest at the annual rate ÷ 12, then your payment comes off. The last payment is just what's left.

**Fixed payment:** months = −ln(1 − balance × r ÷ payment) ÷ ln(1 + r), rounded up, where r is the monthly rate as a decimal. The payment must be bigger than the first month's interest.

**Target date:** payment = balance × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n), where n is the number of months. At 0%, it's the balance ÷ n.

Card companies usually add interest daily, and the minimum payment on a real card falls with the balance, so your statements can differ a little. The calculator assumes a steady rate, no fees and no new spending.

## Tips to clear your card

1. **Choose a fixed payment, above the minimum.** Tell your card company the amount; it has to let you pay any regular sum you like.
2. **Keep the payment when the balance falls.** A fixed payment reduces the balance faster every month, even if you can't raise it.
3. **Reply if your lender writes about persistent debt.** Ignoring it can mean the card is suspended or cancelled.
4. **Compare a personal loan.** A fixed-rate loan at a lower rate can cut the interest. Our [UK loan calculator](/uk/loan-calculator/) shows the payment, but check the fees.
5. **Pay off the highest rate first.** With several cards, extra money saves most there.
6. **Get free help early.** If the payments mostly cover interest, talk to your card company or a free debt adviser, such as MoneyHelper, StepChange or Citizens Advice.
