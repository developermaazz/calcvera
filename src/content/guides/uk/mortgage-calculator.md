## How to use this calculator

The results update as you type.

- **Your property and mortgage:** the property price, your deposit, the mortgage term and whether you'll repay capital and interest or pay interest only.
- **Your deal:** the initial interest rate and how many years it lasts, then the rate you'll move to when it ends. The initial rate starts close to the Bank of England's average effective rate on new secured lending to households, which was 4.63% in September 2026. Use the rates from your mortgage illustration or offer if you have one.
- **Fees:** any product fee, and whether you'll pay it upfront or add it to the mortgage.

The results show your monthly payment during the deal and after it, your mortgage amount, loan to value, total interest and total to repay. A table shows what the deal itself costs and what you'll still owe when it ends. Below the calculator, the chart shows the capital and interest you pay each year, and the schedule gives the figures by year or by month.

## Example: £300,000 home, 5-year fix

Say you buy a £300,000 home with a £45,000 deposit (15%) and borrow £255,000 over 25 years. You take a 5-year fixed rate of 4.6% with a £999 product fee, paid upfront. When the fix ends, your lender's rate is 7.1%.

- **During the fix:** £1,431.89 a month. The first payment is £977.50 of interest and £454.39 of capital.
- **What the 5 years cost:** £85,913.13 in payments, of which £55,325.47 is interest, or £86,912.13 with the fee. You'd still owe £224,412.34 when the fix ends.
- **After the fix:** £1,753.36 a month for the remaining 20 years, £321.48 more than before, if you stay on the 7.1% rate.
- **Over 25 years:** £251,720.10 of interest. If you could keep 4.6% for the whole term, it would be £174,565.65, which is £77,154.45 less. Future rates are unknown, but this shows why the end of a deal matters.

Here's what changing one thing at a time does:

| Change | During the deal | After the deal | Total interest |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The example above | £1,431.89 | £1,753.36 | £251,720.10 |
| A 2-year deal instead of 5 | £1,431.89 | £1,793.30 | £274,315.72 |
| 10% deposit (£30,000) | £1,516.11 | £1,856.50 | £266,527.17 |
| A 30-year term | £1,307.24 | £1,660.28 | £321,519.46 |
| Interest-only | £977.50 | £1,508.75 | £420,750.00 |

On interest-only you'd also still owe the full £255,000 at the end of the term.

## How it's calculated

**Repayment mortgage:**

**M = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n)**

where **M** is the monthly payment, **P** the amount borrowed, **r** the monthly rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and **n** the number of monthly payments. At 4.6%, r = 0.383%.

Each month, interest is the balance × r, and the rest of the payment repays capital. When the deal ends, the payment is worked out again with the same formula, using the balance left, the new rate and the months remaining. The last payment is adjusted so the balance ends at exactly £0.

**Interest-only:** each payment is the amount borrowed × r. The balance doesn't go down, so the whole amount is due at the end of the term.

**Loan to value** = (price − deposit) ÷ price.

**Deal cost** = the payments during the deal + the product fee if you pay it upfront. A fee added to the mortgage is charged interest like the rest of the loan instead.

The calculator assumes each rate stays the same for its period. Some lenders work out interest daily, so your lender's figures may differ by a few pounds. Figures are calculated at full precision and rounded for display.

## Tips for your mortgage

1. **Diary the end of your deal.** In the example, the payment would rise by £321.48 a month on the follow-on rate. Your lender or a broker can tell you when you can start looking for a new deal.
2. **Compare the total cost, not just the rate.** A lower rate with a big fee can cost more over the deal than a slightly higher rate with no fee. Use the deal cost to compare.
3. **A bigger deposit lowers your LTV.** In the example, a 10% deposit instead of 15% adds £84.22 a month during the deal and £14,807.07 of interest overall.
4. **A longer term lowers the payment but costs more.** Going from 25 to 30 years saves £124.65 a month during the deal, but adds £69,799.36 of interest.
5. **Have a plan for an interest-only loan.** You'll need a credible way to repay the full amount at the end, such as savings or investments.
6. **Budget for the costs of buying.** Stamp duty, legal fees and surveys come on top of your deposit. Our [UK stamp duty calculator](/uk/stamp-duty-calculator/) works out the tax for England and Northern Ireland.
