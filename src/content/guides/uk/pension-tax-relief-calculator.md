## How to use this calculator

The results update as you type.

- **Yearly salary before tax:** your pay before any pension deduction. Enter 0 if you have no earnings.
- **Where you live:** Scottish taxpayers have different Income Tax rates, which change the relief.
- **How you pay into your pension:** relief at source, a net pay arrangement or salary sacrifice. Your payslip or pension provider can tell you which one you have.
- **How much you pay in:** a percentage of your salary, or an amount each month. Enter the total going into your pension from you, including relief. If you pay £80 into a relief at source pension, that's £100.
- **Your employer pays in:** optional, but it's checked against your annual allowance.

The results show your tax relief for the year, how much goes into your pension each month, and what it really costs you. A table compares the same contribution under all three methods. Below the calculator, the chart shows where the money in your pension comes from.

## Example: 5% of a £60,000 salary

Say you earn £60,000 in England and pay 5% (£3,000 a year) into a personal pension that uses relief at source. Your employer adds 3% (£1,800).

You pay £2,400 from your take-home pay, and your provider adds £600 of basic-rate relief to make £3,000. Because £9,730 of your income is taxed at 40%, all £3,000 of the contribution qualifies for higher-rate relief, so you can claim another £600 from HMRC. Your total relief is £1,200, and the £3,000 contribution costs you £1,800.

| Same £3,000 contribution by | Costs you a year | Relief |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Relief at source | £1,800.00 | £1,200.00 |
| Net pay arrangement | £1,800.00 | £1,200.00 |
| Salary sacrifice | £1,740.00 | £1,260.00 |

Salary sacrifice saves an extra £60 of National Insurance (2% on earnings above £50,270).

Other cases:

- **£35,000 salary, 5% (£1,750):** £350 of relief at 20%, or £490 with salary sacrifice, which also saves 8% National Insurance.
- **£110,000 salary, £10,000 in:** £6,000 of relief, an effective 60%, because the contribution restores £5,000 of Personal Allowance.
- **£50,000 salary in Scotland, £2,500 in:** £1,050 of relief, at 42%.
- **£10,000 salary, £500 in:** relief at source adds £100, but a net pay arrangement gives no relief, because you don't pay Income Tax.

## How it's calculated

**Relief at source:** your provider adds 20% on contributions up to 100% of your earnings, or up to £3,600 gross if you earn less. Where your income is taxed above 20%, the extra relief is the tax you'd save on the slice of income your contribution covers. You claim that part from HMRC. In Scotland, extra relief is 1% on income taxed at 21%, 22% at 42%, 25% at 45% and 28% at 48%. If your top rate is 19%, you still keep the full 20%.

**Net pay arrangement:** relief = Income Tax on your salary − Income Tax on (salary − contribution).

**Salary sacrifice:** the same Income Tax saving, plus the employee National Insurance you don't pay: 8% between £12,570 and £50,270, and 2% above.

The Personal Allowance taper (£1 lost for every £2 over £100,000) and Scottish bands are built in. The calculator also checks everything going in, including your employer's contributions, against the 2026 to 2027 **annual allowance** of £60,000. It reduces by £1 for every £2 of adjusted income over £260,000 (when threshold income is over £200,000), to a minimum of £10,000, or is £10,000 after flexible access.

Figures are calculated at full precision and rounded for display.

## Tips to get the most from pension tax relief

1. **Claim your higher-rate relief.** If you pay 40% or 45% tax and your pension uses relief at source, the extra isn't added automatically. Claim it through Self Assessment or by contacting HMRC.
2. **Ask about salary sacrifice.** If your employer offers it, the National Insurance saving makes each pound you pay in cheaper. Some employers also add their own NI savings to your pension.
3. **Use the £100,000 to £125,140 band.** Contributions here get an effective 60% relief and can win back your Personal Allowance.
4. **Make the most of your employer's contribution.** If your employer matches extra contributions, paying in up to the match is often the best value available.
5. **Watch the annual allowance.** High earners and anyone who has taken money flexibly should check both their allowance and any unused allowance from the last 3 tax years before making large contributions.
6. **On a low income, check your scheme type.** If you don't pay Income Tax and your workplace pension uses net pay, ask whether relief at source is available.
