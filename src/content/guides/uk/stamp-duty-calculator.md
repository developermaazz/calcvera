## How to use this calculator

The result updates as you type.

- **Property price:** the price you've agreed for a home in England or Northern Ireland.
- **Which describes you?** Choose moving home or buying your only home, first-time buyer, or buying an additional property such as a second home or buy-to-let.
- **Not a UK resident:** tick this if any buyer isn't UK resident for Stamp Duty purposes.

The result shows the Stamp Duty you'd pay and the effective rate as a share of the price. If you're a first-time buyer, it also shows how much the relief saves you. Below the calculator, a table shows the tax on each band.

## Example: a £295,000 home

Say you're buying a £295,000 home in England.

| Price band | Rate | Amount in band | Tax |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Up to £125,000 | 0% | £125,000 | £0 |
| £125,001 to £250,000 | 2% | £125,000 | £2,500 |
| £250,001 to £925,000 | 5% | £45,000 | £2,250 |
| **Total** | | | **£4,750** |

That's 1.61% of the price. Here's how the same price changes with your situation:

| You are | Stamp Duty |
| --- | --- |
| Moving home or buying your only home | £4,750 |
| First-time buyer | £0 |
| Buying an additional property | £19,500 |
| Not a UK resident (only home) | £10,650 |

First-time buyers should watch the £500,000 limit. At £500,000, a first-time buyer pays £10,000. At £510,000, the relief disappears completely and the bill jumps to £15,500, an extra £5,500 for a home that costs £10,000 more. If you're close to the limit, it can be worth negotiating the price below it.

## How it's calculated

Stamp Duty is charged in bands, like Income Tax. Each rate applies only to the part of the price that falls in its band.

**Standard rates:**

- 0% up to £125,000
- 2% from £125,001 to £250,000
- 5% from £250,001 to £925,000
- 10% from £925,001 to £1.5 million
- 12% above £1.5 million

**First-time buyers:** 0% up to £300,000 and 5% from £300,001 to £500,000. Above £500,000, the standard rates apply to the whole price.

**Surcharges:** buying an additional property adds 5 percentage points to every band, unless the price is under £40,000. Buyers who aren't UK resident pay a further 2 percentage points on every band, for purchases of £40,000 or more. Both surcharges can apply together.

The calculator covers a single residential property bought as one purchase. It doesn't cover shared ownership, mixed-use or non-residential property, or other reliefs.

## Tips to plan for Stamp Duty

1. **Check whether you count as a first-time buyer.** Everyone you're buying with must be a first-time buyer to get the relief.
2. **Budget for it early.** Stamp Duty is usually due on completion, on top of your deposit, so include it when you work out how much you need.
3. **Mind the £500,000 first-time buyer limit.** Going even slightly over it removes the whole relief.
4. **Keep records if you're moving home.** If you pay the higher rates because your old home hasn't sold yet, you can usually reclaim the extra if you sell it within 3 years.
5. **Ask your conveyancer to confirm the figure.** They file the return and pay the tax, and can check whether any other relief applies to your purchase.
