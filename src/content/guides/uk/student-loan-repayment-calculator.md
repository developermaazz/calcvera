## How to use this calculator

The results update as you type.

- **Your loan plan:** Plan 1, 2, 4 or 5, or a Postgraduate Loan. You can check your plan in your online repayment account.
- **Loan balance:** what you owe today, including interest added so far.
- **Yearly salary before tax:** your current gross pay.
- **Expected yearly pay rise:** how much you expect your salary to grow each year.
- **Years since you were first due to repay:** used to count down to the write-off date. Enter 0 if your first repayments start this April.

The results show your monthly repayment now, the interest rate, whether you'll repay the loan or have it written off and when, the total you'll repay and any amount written off. The chart and table show your balance year by year.

## Example: £45,000 on Plan 2

Say you have £45,000 of Plan 2 debt, earn £35,000, and expect 3% pay rises each year.

- **Monthly repayment now:** 9% of the £5,615 above the £29,385 threshold, or **£42.11 a month**.
- **Interest rate now:** 4.55%, because Plan 2 interest rises with income above £29,385.
- **Outcome:** you'd repay £70,523 over 30 years, and the remaining £97,648 would be written off.

Early on, the interest added each year is more than you repay, so the balance grows even while you pay. For many Plan 2 borrowers, the loan works more like a 9% tax on earnings above the threshold, for up to 30 years, than a normal debt.

Compare a higher earner. On £60,000 with the same balance, repayments start at £229.61 a month and the loan is cleared in 19 years. They'd repay £80,071 in total, more than the person on £35,000, but they'd be free of it 11 years sooner.

Other plans work differently:

- **Plan 1:** £15,000 left, earning £32,000, 5 years into repaying: £38.25 a month, cleared in 18 more years.
- **Postgraduate Loan:** £12,000, earning £40,000: £95 a month, cleared in 12 years.

## How it's calculated

1. **Repayment** = 9% × (salary − plan threshold), or 6% over £21,000 for a Postgraduate Loan, worked out monthly.
2. **Interest** is added each month at your plan's yearly rate ÷ 12. Plan 2's rate rises in a straight line from 4.1% at £29,385 to 6% at £52,885.
3. Each year, your salary rises by the pay rise you entered.
4. Repayments continue until the balance reaches zero or the write-off date: 25 years for Plan 1, 30 years for Plans 2 and 4 and Postgraduate, and 40 years for Plan 5, counted from the April you were first due to repay.

The calculator keeps today's thresholds and interest rates for every future year, but both change over time. Interest rates are usually updated each September. Older Plan 1 and Plan 4 loans, taken out before September 2006 and August 2007, are written off at 65 instead, which isn't modelled here.

## Tips for managing your student loan

1. **Check your plan and balance.** Your online repayment account shows both, and they decide everything else.
2. **Check your payslip.** Make sure your employer is taking repayments for the right plan.
3. **Think before overpaying.** If you're unlikely to clear the loan before it's written off, extra payments may not save you money.
4. **Use real pay rises.** The result depends heavily on how your income grows. Try a few different rates.
5. **Keep your contact details up to date.** You'll get statements and can check that repayments have been recorded.
