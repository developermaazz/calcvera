## How to use this calculator

The results update as you type.

- **Yearly salary before tax:** your gross pay for the year, including bonuses and overtime you expect.
- **Where you live:** Scottish taxpayers pay Scottish Income Tax rates. Everyone else, including Wales, uses the rest-of-UK rates.
- **Your pension contribution:** the percentage of your salary you pay into a workplace pension.
- **How your pension is paid:** net pay (taken before tax), relief at source (taken after tax, with tax relief added by your provider) or salary sacrifice. Your payslip or pension scheme will say which.
- **Student loan:** your plan, and whether you also have a Postgraduate Loan.

The results show your monthly, yearly and weekly take-home pay, and each deduction. Below the calculator, a chart and table show where your pay goes, by year, month and week.

## Example: £35,000 with a 5% pension

Say you earn £35,000 in England, pay 5% into a net pay pension and have no student loan.

| | Year | Month |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gross pay | £35,000.00 | £2,916.67 |
| Pension (5%) | − £1,750.00 | − £145.83 |
| Income Tax | − £4,136.00 | − £344.67 |
| National Insurance | − £1,794.40 | − £149.53 |
| **Take-home pay** | **£27,319.60** | **£2,276.63** |

Because the pension comes off before tax, you pay Income Tax on £33,250 rather than £35,000. If your employer offered salary sacrifice instead, you'd also save National Insurance on the £1,750, and take home £140 more a year.

A few other cases:

- **Living in Scotland:** on £35,000 with no pension, Income Tax is £4,501.07, just £15.07 more than in England. At £60,000, the gap grows to £1,750.05, because Scotland's higher rate is 42% and starts at a lower income.
- **Plan 2 student loan:** at £35,000, you'd repay £505.35 a year, 9% of the £5,615 above the threshold.
- **Earning £110,000:** your Personal Allowance falls to £7,570. Paying 10% (£11,000) into a pension by salary sacrifice restores the full allowance, so your take-home pay falls by only £4,380.

That last point is one generic articles often miss: between £100,000 and £125,140, each extra £1 of salary loses 50p of allowance, and pension contributions can win it back.

## How it's calculated

1. **Pension:** salary sacrifice reduces your pay before tax, National Insurance and student loan. Net pay reduces the pay you're taxed on only. Relief at source comes from your take-home pay: you pay 80% and your provider adds the 20% basic-rate relief.
2. **Personal Allowance:** £12,570, reduced by £1 for every £2 of adjusted net income over £100,000.
3. **Income Tax:** the rest of your taxable pay is taxed at 20%, 40% and 45% in the rest of the UK, or 19%, 20%, 21%, 42%, 45% and 48% in Scotland, using the 2026/27 bands.
4. **National Insurance:** 8% on earnings between £12,570 and £50,270, and 2% above.
5. **Student loan:** 9% of pay over your plan's threshold, plus 6% over £21,000 for a Postgraduate Loan.

The calculator uses yearly figures and the standard tax code. Payroll works per pay period, so payslips can differ by a few pounds. It doesn't include Marriage Allowance, taxable benefits, or extra pension relief claimed through Self Assessment.

## Tips to keep more of your pay

1. **Check your tax code.** A wrong code is a common reason for paying too much tax. It's on your payslip and in your HMRC online account.
2. **Ask about salary sacrifice.** If your employer offers it, you save National Insurance on your pension contributions.
3. **Claim higher-rate pension relief.** If you pay into a relief at source scheme and pay tax above the basic rate, you can claim the extra relief through Self Assessment.
4. **Watch the £100,000 taper.** Pension contributions or Gift Aid donations can reduce the income used to work out your Personal Allowance.
5. **Plan for pay rises.** Use the calculator to see how much of a rise you'll actually take home after tax, National Insurance and student loan.
