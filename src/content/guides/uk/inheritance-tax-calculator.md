## How to use this calculator

The result updates as you type.

- **Total value of the estate:** everything the person owns at death, including their home or share of it, savings, investments and possessions.
- **Debts and funeral costs:** mortgages, loans, unpaid bills and reasonable funeral costs. These come off first.
- **Gifts in the last 7 years:** gifts that count for Inheritance Tax after exemptions such as the annual exemption. They use up the nil-rate band first.
- **Leaving a home to children or grandchildren:** tick this to apply the residence nil-rate band, then enter the value of the home (or share) less any mortgage on it.
- **Marital status:** if married, enter the amount left to the spouse or civil partner, which is exempt. If widowed, enter the share of each band your late spouse didn't use.
- **Share left to charity:** the percentage of the estate (after debts) going to charity. Charity gifts are exempt and can bring the rate down to 36%.

## Example: a £750,000 estate with a home

Say an estate is worth £750,000, with £15,000 of debts and funeral costs. The home, worth £350,000, goes to the children. The person was single.

| | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Estate after debts | £735,000 |
| Nil-rate band | − £325,000 |
| Residence nil-rate band | − £175,000 |
| Taxable amount | £235,000 |
| Inheritance Tax at 40% | **£94,000** |

Now change one thing at a time:

- **Widowed, with both bands unused by the late spouse:** the tax-free bands double to £1 million, and no Inheritance Tax is due.
- **10% to charity (£73,500):** that's more than 10% of the £410,000 baseline amount, so the rate falls to 36% and the tax drops to £58,140. The charity receives £73,500, but the family receives only £37,640 less than before, because the tax falls by £35,860.
- **5% to charity (£36,750):** below the 10% test, so the 40% rate stays and the tax is £79,300.

For larger estates, the taper matters. With a £2.3 million estate (after £15,000 of debts) and a £900,000 home, the residence band falls by £142,500 to £32,500, and the tax is £771,000.

## How it's calculated

**Tax = (estate − exemptions − nil-rate band available − residence band used) × rate**

1. **Net estate** = total value − debts and funeral costs.
2. **Exemptions:** anything left to a spouse, civil partner or charity.
3. **Nil-rate band available** = £325,000, plus any transferred share from a late spouse, minus gifts in the last 7 years.
4. **Residence nil-rate band** = £175,000 (plus any transferred share) if a home goes to direct descendants, reduced by £1 for every £2 the net estate is over £2 million, and limited to the home's value.
5. **Rate** = 40%, or 36% if charity gifts are at least 10% of the baseline amount. Following HMRC's method, the baseline amount is the estate after exemptions minus the nil-rate band, with the charity gifts added back.

The calculator doesn't cover tax on the gifts themselves, taper relief on gifts, Business Relief, Agricultural Relief or the downsizing addition.

## Tips for planning Inheritance Tax

1. **Make or update your will.** It decides who receives what, and whether the spouse and residence bands are used fully.
2. **Keep records of gifts.** Executors need to know about gifts made in the 7 years before death.
3. **Think about the home.** The residence nil-rate band only applies when a home goes to direct descendants, including step-children and adopted children.
4. **Check the charity test before writing a legacy.** Leaving 10% of the baseline amount can cost your family much less than it gives to charity, as the example shows.
5. **Get professional advice for complex estates.** Trusts, business assets and farms have rules this calculator doesn't cover.
